# m8_measurement: Input measurement and flexible inputs

**Question.** Can the Kaplan–Chinchilla discrepancy in the allocation exponent a (N* ∝ C^a) be read as two classic IO problems?
- **Input measurement.** Nerlove 1963 [nerlove1963returns]; Christensen & Greene 1976 [christensen1976economies]; Collard-Wexler & De Loecker 2016 [collardwexler2016production].
- **Flexible inputs that are not concentrated out.** Hyperparameters as flexible inputs [gandhi2020identification; ackerberg2015identification]. Inefficiency correlated with the inputs, as in stochastic frontiers [aigner1977formulation; meeusen1977efficiency; caudill1995frontier].

For each problem the memo asks: how much of the gap it explains, whether a formula predicts that amount, and which scaling-law objects survive.

**Reproducibility.** Every number here is regenerated by one command, `code/analysis/m8_measurement/run.py`:
- It uses fixed seeds and at most 6 processes. The builder's end-to-end run took 35.5 minutes on the heavily loaded machine (load average 40–50). The reviewer's clean reruns (outputs deleted first) took 26.8 minutes (original code; every CSV, TeX and JSON output bit-identical) and 17–24 minutes with the review fixes, depending on load.
- The machine-readable summary is `data/processed/m8_measurement/results_summary.json`.

**Review status.** This memo was audited and revised by the independent reviewer on 2026-09-23 (see `output/memos/m8_measurement_review.md`). Claims 7 and 11 were substantively revised and claim 8 and the Step Law results were qualified; the changes are marked "review" in the text.

**Tags.** [E] = estimated here. [A] = assumed. [L] = taken from the literature, not re-estimated. Notation follows `paper/notes/model_spec.md`.

### 1.3 Measurement bias formula (Proposition M) [E; derived and verified]
**Setup.** Let the true parameter count be N = N_m + X(N_m), where N_m is the measured count. The omitted component X has share s = X/N and scale elasticity θ = dln X/dln N_m < 1. The researcher uses C_m = 6 N_m D and takes the IsoFLOP argmin in measured units.

**Result.** The researcher's first-order condition is α u κ = β v, with κ = 1 − s(1 − θ). The local measured exponent is

  a_m = β / (β + ακ − η),   η = dln κ / dln N_m = (1 − θ) s (κ − θ)/κ ≥ 0.

**Properties:**
- a_m ≥ a = β/(α+β), with equality only when s = 0 or θ = 1.
- A proportional error (θ = 1) only moves the level G. The bias comes entirely from the part of the error that is correlated with the regressor, exactly as in Collard-Wexler and De Loecker (2016).
- As s → 1, a_m → β/(β + αθ), which is Pearce & Song's small-scale limit.
- a_m is **non-monotone**. It peaks at 0.98 (Besiroglu) to 0.99 (Hoffmann) where the embedding share is about 0.75 (N_m ≈ 2M with Pearce–Song's ω). Figure 1(c) shows this.
- This is Nerlove's lesson: a local Cobb–Douglas elasticity estimated on a restricted size range is not structural.

**Verification:**
- sympy: the implicit derivative minus the closed form simplifies to 0.
- Numerical: the maximum absolute error against the numerical derivative of the exact path is 1e-4. Against brute-force argmins, the error in ln N is below 2e-7.

**Application to Pearce–Song [pearce2024reconciling].** We use their embedding map, N_T = N_E + ω N_E^{1/3} with ω = 47,491 [L]. Over Kaplan's range (N_E from 790 to 1.58e9), the measured a is:
- Besiroglu parameters [besiroglu2024chinchilla]: **0.792** (true a 0.513). Pearce & Song report 0.78.
- Hoffmann parameters [hoffmann2022training]: **0.757** at TeX precision and 0.752 rounded (true a 0.456–0.452). Pearce & Song report 0.74.

**Application to Porian's architecture ladder.** Here the omitted component is the head.
- The head share falls from 0.94 (5M parameters) to 0.084 (901M) [E].
- The fitted head map is X = ω N_m^θ with θ = 0.358 and ω = 48,210 [E].
- A semi-synthetic check replaces every loss with a smooth technology fitted to the same runs, then reruns the pipeline on the identical design. It predicts measurement biases of 0.208, 0.170, 0.214 and 0.158. Refitting the technology with E held at 1.8 gives 0.188, 0.162, 0.212 and 0.121.
- The observed biases are 0.137, 0.118, 0.166 and 0.129 (RW Kaplan setup, RW tuned, OWT2 Kaplan setup, OWT2 tuned).
- The pure closed form, applied to the same fitted technologies along the continuous researcher path over the same measured-compute range (i.e., without Porian's discrete ladder and edge-argmin rule), predicts larger biases: 0.285, 0.199, 0.270 and 0.224 (`_porian_meas_formula.csv`, column `delta_pred`).
- **So the formula gets the sign and order of magnitude right and over-predicts: by 20–50% in the semi-synthetic check on the actual design, and by 63–108% with the continuous closed form.**

**Re-estimating the Chinchilla extraction with non-embedding N** [E]:
- Besiroglu sample (n = 240), Huber-LSE estimator, 200 pairs-bootstrap draws.
- a moves from 0.514 (SE 0.020) with total N to **0.556** (SE 0.023) with non-embedding N.
- The paired difference is **+0.042** (SE 0.006, 95% CI [0.030, 0.050]).
- σ* moves from 0.737 to 0.753 (paired SE of the difference 0.002).
- The effect is modest because embeddings are only 0.7–28.6% of parameters in this sample.
- On small-model subsamples the shift is larger: +0.069 for N_T < 1e9 (n = 118) and +0.082 for N_T < 3e8 (n = 54). These are point estimates only.

### 1.4 Flexible-input bias formula (Proposition F) [E; derived and verified]
**Setup.** Let observed loss be L_obs = L*·e^{ι(N,D)}, where L* is the concentrated technology and ι ≥ 0 is the inefficiency of the hyperparameter policy. We write ι rather than the SFA-conventional u because the model spec reserves u = A N^−α and v = B D^−β; the gradients are ι_n = ∂ι/∂ln N and ι_d = ∂ι/∂ln D.

**Result.** On an IsoFLOP the researcher's first-order condition is α u − β v = L·(ι_n − ι_d). To first order, the log argmin shifts by

  −Δ(C)/f''(C),   where Δ = ∂(L_obs − L*)/∂ln N at fixed C, and f'' = (α+β)γR* is the curvature of the concentrated IsoFLOP curve.

Hence **a_obs − a = −∂_{ln C}[Δ/f'']**. With constant log-gradients this becomes a_obs − a = −(ι_n − ι_d)·E/((α+β)R*(C)).

**Implications:**
- **Only the transverse gradient ι_n − ι_d matters for a.** Inefficiency that depends on (N, D) only through C leaves a unbiased.
- The bias in a is first order in the inefficiency gradient, even though the bias in the loss level is second order in the hyperparameter error (the envelope theorem).

**Simulation check** (`m8_measurement_ineff_formula_check.csv`). With the Besiroglu technology, the formula matches simulated IsoFLOP argmins to within 0.003 in a (worst case at gradients of 0.01). A parametric fit with E fixed behaves the same way: transverse gradients move a and leave σ* almost unchanged, while along-path gradients (ι_n = ι_d = ±0.003) move σ* by ±0.010 and a by less than 0.001.

**Applied to Porian's runs.** δ(N, C) is the excess loss of an untuned step over the tuned step, on matched (N, C) cells. Using f'' from the fitted tuned technology, the formula predicts:

| Comparison with step 5 (tuned) | RW observed | RW predicted | OWT2 observed | OWT2 predicted |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Step 2 | 0.209 | 0.174 (83%) | 0.183 | 0.198 (108%) |
| Step 3 | 0.106 | 0.098 (92%) | 0.085 | 0.094 (110%) |
| Step 4 | 0.075 | 0.068 (91%) | 0.057 | 0.065 (114%) |

- With a kernel-local curvature instead, the predictions explain 47–62% (bandwidth 1) or 60–83% (bandwidth 0.5). **So the share explained is 47–114% depending on how the curvature f'' is estimated**; the two f'' estimates differ by a factor of up to 4.4 at individual budgets.
- **Mechanism.** The untuned runs' excess loss is enormous at small budgets. At C = 2.5e16, δ = 1.57 nats at the tuned optimum, because Kaplan's warmup of 1.57B tokens and the 0.5M-token batch leave too few useful steps for short runs. The excess vanishes by C ≈ 3e18. So Δ > 0 at small C pushes the small-budget argmins down by 0.7–1.2 log points, and the fitted slope steepens.

### 1.5 Step Law: hyperparameters as flexible inputs [E]
Data: 1,911 runs in 17 (N, D) cells [li2025predictablea]. 9.5% of runs diverged.

**(i) The concentrated technology does not identify a.**
- The Chinchilla fit on the 17 grid minima is fragile:
  - Huber-LSE (the paper's reference estimator): E = 0.91, a = 0.689, σ* = 0.824.
  - NLS on log loss: E = 1.43, a = 0.563, σ* = 0.786.
  - Residual-bootstrap SEs for the NLS fit (200 draws; residuals centred and rescaled by √(n/(n−5)), a review fix that raised the SEs by about 20%): E 0.50, a 0.149, σ* 0.040.
- The profile in E is flat: the sum of squared residuals varies by less than 17% for E between 0.6 and 1.7. Over that range a runs from 0.762 to 0.454 and σ* from 0.827 to 0.732 (Figure 2(b)–(c)); α+β runs from 0.417 to 0.732 and the frontier exponent γ from 0.076 to 0.181 (a factor of 2.4). See (vii) on what this does and does not say about the relative stability of σ* and a.
- **Units of N (review addition).** Step Law's N is the *non-embedding* count, Kaplan's convention. By Proposition M the levels of a in this subsection are therefore biased upward. Counting the head (N + 65,536·h, head share 11–23%; vocabulary [L]) moves the NLS frontier fit from a = 0.563 to 0.465 (E free; σ* 0.786 → 0.752) and from 0.573 to 0.550 at E = 1.4; also counting untied input embeddings [A] gives 0.405 and 0.531 (`m8_measurement_steplaw_count.csv`). Only *differences across policies at fixed E and fixed N convention* are used below.

**(ii) Flexible-input policies at E fixed at 1.4.**
- At E = 1.4 the frontier gives a = 0.573 (residual-bootstrap SE 0.108) and σ* = 0.790 (SE 0.014). Relative to that, rule-based policies shift a by between −0.041 and +0.076:

| Policy | Shift in a |
|---|---|
| Step Law rule | −0.041 |
| DeepSeek C-rule [bi2024deepseek] | −0.031 |
| Bjorck (N, D) rule [bjorck2024scaling] | +0.003 |
| Best single fixed (LR, batch) | +0.046 |
| Porian "base" (LR 3e-3, 0.5M batch) | +0.047 |
| Porian N-rule | +0.076 |

- σ* moves by at most 0.010 under any rule. The shifts are similar at E = 1.2 and 1.6: a moves by −0.036 to +0.070, and σ* stays within 0.013 of the frontier.
- All these rules are cheap: their mean inefficiency is only 0.1–0.9% of loss.
- Relative to the residual-bootstrap SEs, the largest a-shift is 0.70 SE and the largest σ*-shift 0.74 SE: in SE units the two objects are equally (in)sensitive to the policy. The 17-cell design cannot statistically separate policy bias from design noise.

**(iii) Random configurations** (Monte Carlo, 200 draws per k: each cell tries k non-diverged configurations and keeps the best; E = 1.4). For a, the main effect of untuned configurations is lost *precision*; its median bias is small. For σ*, a single random configuration per cell also produces a sizeable median *bias*.

| k | Mean inefficiency | Median a (MC s.d.) | Median σ* (MC s.d.) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2.58% | 0.539 (0.266) | 0.739 (0.096) |
| 4 | 0.70% | 0.555 (0.139) | 0.786 (0.025) |
| 16 | 0.21% | 0.558 (0.041) | 0.791 (0.005) |

- Median biases relative to the frontier (a 0.573, σ* 0.790): a −0.034 / −0.019 / −0.015 and σ* −0.051 / −0.004 / +0.001 for k = 1 / 4 / 16. In units of the frontier's residual-bootstrap SE, the k = 1 bias is −0.3 SE for a but −3.7 SE for σ*.
- At k = 16 the coefficient of variation of a (7%) is about 11 times that of σ* (0.6%), but only about twice that of α+β (3.3%), the parameter σ* is a monotone transform of. At k = 1 the CV of α+β (76%) exceeds that of a (49%).

**(iv) Inefficiency is D-biased.**
- For a random non-diverged configuration, OLS of ι with SEs clustered by cell gives ∂ι/∂ln D = −0.0068 (SE 0.0006) and ∂ι/∂ln N = −0.0023 (0.0014); mean ι is 2.65%.
- A heteroskedastic stochastic frontier (cell fixed effects; the log of the inefficiency scale σ_u linear in ln N and ln D) gives the same picture:

| SFA variant | γ_D (SE) | γ_N (SE) |
|---|---|---|
| Half-normal | −0.231 (0.020) [0.032] | −0.038 (0.037) [0.076] |
| Exponential (fits better: −log L = −4,585 vs −4,463) | −0.260 (0.028) [0.023] | −0.063 (0.053) [0.071] |

(Parentheses: numerical-Hessian SEs. Brackets: cluster-robust sandwich SEs by cell, 17 clusters, a review addition. The last row is a transparent cross-check; for exponential ι, E[ι] = σ_u.)

- **Longer training horizons make the loss landscape flatter in (LR, batch).**
- Plugging the random-configuration gradients into Proposition F (constant-gradient case, E = 1.4) gives an IsoFLOP bias in a of **−0.012** for a researcher using random configurations.
- The SFA frontier coincides with the grid minimum: the mean absolute gap is 0.00–0.11%. In the half-normal model σ_v → 0, because dispersion within the grid is one-sided.

**(v) Flexible-input demand functions** (17 cells). Step Law's published values [L] are LR: N −0.713, D +0.307; batch: D 0.571.

*Learning rate:*

| Specification | N elasticity (SE) | D elasticity (SE) |
|---|---|---|
| Grid argmin | −0.823 (0.127) | +0.288 (0.071) |
| Smoothed argmin | −0.693 (0.050) | +0.210 (0.025) |

- Their 1,000 bootstrap fits give s.d. 0.030 on the N elasticity and 0.019 on the D elasticity.

*Batch size, in tokens:*

| Specification | D elasticity (SE) | N elasticity (SE) |
|---|---|---|
| Grid argmin, D only | 0.498 (0.095) | – |
| Smoothed argmin, D only | 0.572 (0.037) | – |
| Smoothed argmin, with N | 0.598 (0.021) | −0.205 (0.041) |

- So Step Law's D-only batch rule omits a negative N-elasticity: significant with smoothed optima (t ≈ −5) and marginal with grid argmins (−0.355, SE 0.176).
- Caveat on the smoothed optima: the fitted quadratic's minimum lies *above* the best observed run in 16 of 17 cells (by up to 0.29%), so the quadratic misfits the sharp, asymmetric optimum; its within-cell SEs understate the uncertainty about the optimum's location. The grid has up to 12 LRs × 10 batch sizes per cell, but the N = 1.07B, D = 56.9B cell has only 5 LRs (47 runs) and its best LR at batch 256 is the largest one tried there.

**(vi) Conditional versus unconditional demand: the Bjorck–Step Law sign puzzle.**
- Holding the batch fixed, the D-elasticity of the optimal LR is **+0.046 (SE 0.015)**. The grid-argmin version gives +0.016 (0.029), and fixing the batch at 0.52M tokens gives +0.040 (0.047). The LR–batch complementarity is ∂ln η*/∂ln B = 0.320 (0.034).
- **Le Chatelier (chain-rule) decomposition** [milgrom1996lechatelier]: conditional elasticity + complementarity × batch elasticity = 0.046 + 0.320 × 0.598 = 0.238, close to the unconditional 0.210. With smoothed optima, batch co-scaling accounts for 91% of Step Law's positive D-elasticity (cell-cluster bootstrap SE 0.15; 90% CI [0.69, 1.18]). **This share is sensitive to how the optimum is located:** with grid argmins it is 56% (SE 0.24; 90% CI [0.36, 0.89]), from 0.016 + 0.295 × 0.543 = 0.176 against an unconditional 0.288.
- Step Law's positive sign is an *unconditional* demand elasticity. Bjorck et al.'s −0.32 is a *conditional* one, at a fixed 0.5M-token batch, fitted on N ≥ 760M with warmup max(1% of steps, 1,000) and cosine decay to 0.1× the peak LR [L; verified against arXiv:2409.19913 during review].
- **What this does and does not resolve.** Conditioning on the batch removes the *positive* sign (conditional D-elasticity +0.02 to +0.05, not significantly different from zero in the grid version). It does *not* produce Bjorck's negative sign: conditioning closes 26% (smoothed; SE 0.02) to 43% (grid; SE 0.13) of the 0.63 gap between the two published elasticities (+0.307 vs −0.32). A residual gap of about 0.34–0.37 remains unexplained; it may come from differences in schedule, warmup, architecture or scale (Step Law uses a fixed 2,000-step warmup and cosine decay to 1e-5).

**(vii) Is σ* more stable than a? (review addition; `m8_measurement_stability.csv`)** In natural units σ* always moves less than a. But σ* = 2/(2+α+β) compresses changes in α+β by σ*²/2 ≈ 0.3, and in the two natural normalizations the advantage largely disappears:

| Source of variation | a | σ* | α+β | γ | a in SE units | σ* in SE units |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E profile, E ∈ [0.6, 1.7] (half-range / midpoint) | 0.25 | 0.06 | 0.27 | 0.41 | 2.8 | 7.0 |
| Policies at E = 1.4 (half-range / midpoint) | 0.10 | 0.009 | 0.04 | 0.04 | 1.1 | 1.1 |
| N counting convention, E free (half-range / midpoint) | 0.16 | 0.04 | 0.16 | 0.15 | 1.5 | 4.4 |
| One random config per cell, MC (CV) | 0.49 | 0.13 | 0.76 | 0.14 | 2.5 | 7.0 |
| Best of 16 random configs, MC (CV) | 0.07 | 0.007 | 0.03 | 0.02 | 0.4 | 0.4 |

("SE units" = full range, or MC s.d., divided by the frontier's residual-bootstrap SE at E = 1.4: 0.108 for a, 0.0137 for σ*.)
- Relative to α+β, a is about as fragile along the E profile and across N conventions, and 1.5–2.5 times as fragile across policies and at k ≥ 4 (at k = 1 it is less fragile than α+β). The frontier exponent γ is *more* fragile than a along the E profile.
- Relative to the estimator's own sampling error, σ* is never more stable than a, and is 2–3 times *less* stable along the E profile, across N conventions and with one random configuration per cell.
- **So these data do not support a general claim that σ* is more robust than a.** What survives is narrower: given E and the N convention, flexible-input policies tilt α against β (moving a) more than they move α+β (Proposition F: transverse vs along-path inefficiency).

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Porian et al. (Part A; `porian.py`)
**Data.** `data/raw/porian/experiment_results.pickle.xz` (MIT licence), 975 runs:
- By dataset: RW 772, OWT2 203.
- By configuration: base 264, tuned 61, sweep 566, seed 84.
- 16 architectures (width 96–1504, depth 3–30), vocabulary 50,432, sequence length 2,048. Batch size is in sequences of 2,048 tokens.

**Counting conventions** (ported from their `data.py`). C is always FLOPs-per-token × tokens in the same convention.
- **kaplan:** non-embedding, no head.
- **standard:** + head, i.e. V·w, because the head costs FLOPs.
- **attention:** + attention FLOPs.
- **total:** our addition, which also counts the input and positional embeddings (the Pearce–Song "total"): N_total = precise non-embedding body + (2,048 + 2·50,432)·w. (Porian's own `params_all` uses the approximate body 12w²d; we do not use it.)

**Pipeline** (re-implemented from their `analysis.py`):
1. At each budget C ∈ {1.25e16·2^i, i = 0..11}, read each run's loss by log-log interpolation along its loss curve. Drop runs whose nearest logged point is more than 10% away from C. The loss is either the smoothed train loss (their proportional window, p = 0.05) or the validation loss.
2. For each N, keep the minimum loss across runs.
3. Interpolate log loss on log N with an Akima spline over 25·(k−1) points and take the argmin; drop edge argmins.
4. Bootstrap by adding noise and re-interpolating: 1,000 draws of Gaussian loss noise with s.d. 0.002 (RW) or 0.01 (OWT2). In their code the loss-dependent noise rule always returns the lower value.
5. Compute the per-budget s.d. of log N*, with their floor and inflation factor.
6. Point estimate: weighted log-OLS of the bootstrap-median N* on C, with weights 1/s.d.². The same regression on each bootstrap index gives the 95% interval.
- Their saturating loss-curve fits are skipped; they are not needed for a. Seeds are fixed per configuration.

**Measurement-only grid.** The same runs (Kaplan setup = base / long warmup / Kaplan schedule; tuned = tuned / short warmup / constant LR), train loss, four counting conventions.

**Checks of the measurement formula:**
- The closed form (`embed.py`) is verified by sympy and numerically.
- Semi-synthetic check: fit a Chinchilla technology to the same runs (Huber-LSE, sl.FAST_GRID, standard count), evaluate it at exactly the observed (architecture, budget) points, and rerun the pipeline in both counts. As robustness, the technology is refitted with E fixed at 1.8.

**Flexible-input formula:**
- Δ(C) is a Gaussian-kernel local linear slope of δ in ln N (bandwidth 0.75) at the tuned optimum.
- f'' comes either from the fitted tuned technology (α²u + β²v) or from a local quadratic (bandwidth 1.0 or 0.5).
- Predicted Δa is the slope, on ln C, of the predicted shift −Δ/f''.

**Quality-weighted head count** (exploratory). We profile N_λ = N_m + λ·head (tuned runs, Huber-LSE). The objective is lowest at λ = 0.75 for both datasets and highest at λ = 0. The profile is very flat between λ = 0.5 and 1.5, where the objective changes by about 0.1%; at λ = 0 it is 1.0% (RW) and 0.4% (OWT2) higher. The parametric a is 0.731 at λ = 0 and 0.529 at λ = 1 (RW).

### 2.2 Chinchilla extraction (Part B; `embed.py`)
- Besiroglu sample via `sl.chinchilla_extraction` (n = 240).
- N_E is obtained by inverting the Pearce–Song map. ω = 47,491 and θ = 1/3 are [L]/[A]: taken from Pearce & Song's fit to Chinchilla's architectures, not re-estimated.
- Full-sample fits: warm start at the published Besiroglu values plus FAST_GRID. This reproduces the DEFAULT_GRID optimum, a = 0.5139.
- Pairs bootstrap: 200 draws, warm start plus a 16-start MINI_GRID. On the first 6 draws, FAST_GRID gives an identical a (maximum difference 0.000).

### 2.3 Step Law (Part C; `steplaw.py`)
**Data.** `dense_lr_bs_loss.csv` (no licence file; we use it but redistribute only cell-level aggregates):
- 17 (N, D) cells: N ∈ {215, 268, 429, 537, 1,074}M non-embedding parameters, D from 4B to 100B, M from 18.6 to 466.
- Up to 12 LRs × 10 batch sizes per cell (the N = 1.07B, D = 56.9B cell has only 5 LRs and 47 runs). The LR is indexed as round(2·log2 LR)/2 because the file spells the same LR in two ways.
- Batch is in sequences of 2,048 tokens (checked: bs × steps × 2,048 = D).
- The loss is `smooth loss`, the smoothed final training loss (single epoch).
- Runs with inefficiency ι = ln L − ln L*_cell > 0.30 are classified as diverged; most have loss ≈ 7.

**Frontier and optima:**
- The frontier is the grid minimum in each cell.
- The smoothed optimum is the minimum of a quadratic in (ln LR, ln B), fitted by OLS to runs within 2% of the cell minimum (35–87 runs per cell). The quadratic is positive definite in all 17 cells.
- Within-cell uncertainty: 200 draws of the quadratic's coefficients.

**Demand functions.** OLS across the 17 cells, with HC1 SEs and a two-level bootstrap (1,000 draws: resample cells, then draw each cell's optimum from its within-cell distribution).

**Conditional LR demand:**
- Unit of observation: a cell × batch slice with at least 4 LRs within 3% of the slice minimum and an interior optimum from a quadratic in ln LR (165 slices).
- Pooled OLS on ln N, ln D and ln B, with SEs clustered by cell (17 clusters).

**Policies.** Each policy picks the nearest grid point (in log2 units) to its target:
- Step Law's own rule.
- The best single fixed pair: LR 1.95e-3, batch 192.
- Porian "base": LR 3e-3, batch 256 sequences.
- Porian's N-rule, with N = N_nonemb + 65,536·h (Step Law's vocabulary [L]).
- DeepSeek's C-rule, with C = 6ND.
- Bjorck's (N, D) rule with N and D in billions, which we checked against their 7B / 1T-token example (predicts about 1.1e-4), and a fixed 0.5M-token batch.

**Technology fits.**
- NLS on log loss (Gaussian QMLE), with E free (DEFAULT_GRID for the frontier; warm start plus FAST_GRID otherwise) or E fixed at 1.2, 1.4 or 1.6.
- We use NLS because, with 17 nearly noise-free points, Huber(1e-3) behaves like L1 and has many local optima. Huber results are reported for the frontier.
- E-fixed fits use a local fitter, `common.fit_efixed`, because of the sl.py issue described in Section 5.
- Frontier uncertainty: residual bootstrap, 200 draws; residuals are centred and rescaled by √(n/(n−5)) before resampling (review fix).
- N-convention robustness (review): the frontier refitted with N + 65,536·h (head) and N + 2·65,536·h (head + untied input embedding); `m8_measurement_steplaw_count.csv`.
- Stability comparison (review): half-range/midpoint (or MC coefficient of variation) of a, σ*, α+β and γ, and spreads in units of the frontier's residual-bootstrap SE; `m8_measurement_stability.csv`.

**Monte Carlo.** R = 200 draws for each k ∈ {1, 4, 16}, warm start plus MINI_GRID. Three draws per k were refitted with FAST_GRID; the maximum difference in a was 0.000.

**Stochastic frontier.** Maximum likelihood (L-BFGS-B, 3 starts) with numerical-Hessian SEs, on the 1,730 non-diverged runs. σ_v is either estimated or calibrated at 0.0017, the median residual s.d. of the within-cell quadratics. Review additions: cluster-robust (by cell) sandwich SEs for the variance function, and a cell-level OLS of ln(mean ι) on ln N, ln D as a transparent check.

**Decomposition inference (review).** Cell-cluster bootstrap (1,000 draws; resample the 17 cells with all their batch slices; recompute the unconditional, batch and conditional regressions) for the chain-rule decomposition, separately for smoothed and grid-argmin optima.

## 4. Claims for the paper

1. **The Kaplan–Chinchilla gap is about 40–47% input measurement and 53–61% flexible inputs, and the two parts roughly add up.**
   - Evidence: `_porian_steps.csv` and `_porian_counting.csv`. RW: measurement 0.132 of 0.341, flexible inputs 0.209. OWT2: 0.162 of 0.344. Counting the head changes a by 0.118–0.166 whether or not runs are tuned; tuning changes a by 0.19–0.23 under every count.
   - Caveat: the step order is Porian's, and shares of a sequential decomposition depend on that order. The near-additivity is what makes the split meaningful. This holds for models of 5M–901M parameters only.
2. **Measurement bias has a closed form. It is driven by the regressor-correlated part of the error, is always upward, and vanishes at scale (Proposition M).**
   - Evidence: sympy check (difference 0); `_pearce_song_sim.csv` gives 0.79 / 0.76 / 0.75, against Pearce–Song's 0.78 / 0.74.
   - Caveat: the embedding map ω, θ is taken from Pearce & Song [L]. On Porian's ladder the formula over-predicts the bias by 20–50% in the semi-synthetic check on the actual design (0.16–0.21 vs observed 0.12–0.17) and by 63–108% along the continuous closed-form path (0.20–0.28). A likely reason is that the smooth technology fitted to 5M–901M runs is itself misspecified, with E above the lowest observed loss (Section 5).
3. **The Chinchilla allocation exponent is itself sensitive to the parameter-count convention.** With non-embedding N it rises by 0.042 (SE 0.006) to 0.556, and by 0.07–0.08 in small-model subsamples.
   - Evidence: `_chinchilla_nonembed.csv`, `results_summary.json:chinchilla_nonembed`.
   - Caveat: this relies on the Pearce–Song map, not on Chinchilla's actual architectures. Subsample numbers are point estimates from 5-parameter fits on 54–188 points.
4. **Scale-correlated flexible-input inefficiency biases the IsoFLOP exponent by a first-order, computable amount.** a_obs − a = −∂_{ln C}[Δ/f'']. Only the transverse gradient ι_n − ι_d matters, and inefficiency that depends only on compute leaves a unbiased.
   - Evidence: the simulation check has maximum error 0.003. On Porian's runs the formula explains 83–114% of the three flexible-input steps with the fitted-technology curvature, and 47–83% with local curvature (`_porian_flex_formula.csv`; Figure 1(b)).
   - Caveat: the prediction depends on the curvature f'', which is poorly pinned down on these data; the two estimates of f'' differ by up to a factor of 4.4 at individual budgets, so the defensible statement is "about half to all of the observed Δa" (47–114%). Per-budget predictions are poor for steps 3 and 4 at 8e17–6e18 FLOPs (see `porian_ineff_by_budget.csv`). The good match is for the *slope*.
5. **The same inefficiency can bias a in either direction.**
   - A penalty on short-horizon runs that fades with C (Kaplan's fixed warmup and large batch) biases a *up*.
   - A penalty with constant log-gradients biases a by −(ι_n − ι_d)E/((α+β)R*): its sign is that of −(ι_n − ι_d), and its size grows as R* → 0 because E/R* grows. Random Step Law configurations have ι_n − ι_d = −0.0023 − (−0.0068) = +0.0045 > 0 (inefficiency falls faster with D than with N), which implies a bias of −0.012.
   - Evidence: Proposition F; `results_summary.json:steplaw_random_implied_isoflop_bias`.
   - Caveat: the −0.012 is a formula-based implication at E = 1.4. It is not estimated from an IsoFLOP design, because Step Law has none.
6. **The Step Law design does not identify a.**
   - Evidence: the profile SSR is flat for E between 0.6 and 1.7 while a runs from 0.76 to 0.45 (`steplaw_E_profiles.csv`; Figure 2(b)). The Huber and NLS frontier fits give a = 0.689 and 0.563.
   - Caveat: this is specific to a design with 5 N values, 3–4 D values per N, and no IsoFLOPs. It illustrates the functional-dependence and design point of the paper; it is not a general result.
7. **(Revised in review.) σ* moves less than a in natural units, but this is mostly the mechanical compression of σ* = 2/(2+α+β); these data do not show that σ* is more robust than a.**
   - Evidence (`m8_measurement_stability.csv`; Section 1.5(vii)). In natural units a moves 3–13 times as much as σ* (E profile: range 0.31 vs 0.10; policies at E = 1.4: 0.12 vs 0.015; MC s.d. at k = 16: 0.041 vs 0.005).
   - But relative to α+β, the parameter σ* transforms, a is about equally fragile along the E profile (half-range/midpoint 0.25 vs 0.27) and across N-counting conventions (0.16 vs 0.16), and 1.5–2.5 times as fragile across policies and at k ≥ 4. γ is *more* fragile than a along the E profile (0.41 vs 0.25).
   - In units of the frontier's own residual-bootstrap SE (a 0.108, σ* 0.0137), σ* is never more stable than a. It is 2–3 times *less* stable along the E profile (7.0 vs 2.8 SE), across N conventions (4.4 vs 1.5 SE) and with one random configuration per cell (MC s.d. 7.0 vs 2.5 SE; median bias −3.7 vs −0.3 SE).
   - What survives: at fixed E and N convention, flexible-input policies tilt α against β (moving a) more than they move α+β (Proposition F: transverse vs along-path inefficiency). Policy shifts are at most 0.70 SE (a) and 0.74 SE (σ*): do not claim statistical significance of the policy shifts.
   - **Implication for the paper:** the SYNTHESIS headline "σ is more stable than the allocation exponent" should not rest on this module. Any stability claim must specify the unit, and the E-profile result argues against it.
8. **For a, untuned configurations mainly cost precision, not accuracy; for σ*, one random configuration per cell also biases it.** With one random configuration per cell the Monte Carlo s.d. of a is 0.27 (σ*: 0.10); with the best of 16 it is 0.04 (0.005). Median biases in a are at most 0.035 (≤ 0.3 SE); the median bias in σ* is −0.051 at k = 1 (−3.7 SE) and negligible for k ≥ 4.
   - Evidence: `steplaw_random_config_mc.csv`, `_steplaw_policies.csv`, `_stability.csv`.
   - Caveat: the random draws come from Step Law's grid, which the authors centred on their predicted optimum. Truly naive configurations would do worse.
9. **Hyperparameter inefficiency is D-biased. Longer runs are less sensitive to (LR, batch); model size barely matters.**
   - Evidence: `_sfa.csv`: γ_D = −0.23 (Hessian SE 0.02; cluster-robust 0.03) half-normal, −0.26 (0.03; 0.02) exponential; a transparent cell-level regression gives −0.27 (0.03). γ_N is not significant in any variant. Cluster OLS gives ∂ι/∂ln D = −0.0068 (0.0006). The SFA frontier equals the grid minimum to within 0.1%.
   - Caveat: the inefficiency distribution is *designed* (it depends on the grid), not behavioural. σ_v collapses to 0 in the half-normal model, a boundary case in which SFA adds nothing to the grid minimum beyond the variance function.
10. **Flexible-input demand functions: Step Law's LR rule is replicated.**
    - Evidence (`_demand.csv`): grid-argmin LR elasticities N −0.82 (0.13) and D +0.29 (0.07), against the published −0.713 and +0.307. The D-elasticity of batch is 0.57 (0.04), against 0.571.
    - Batch demand also has a negative N-elasticity: −0.20 (0.04) with the smoothed optimum (t ≈ −5), −0.36 (0.18) with grid argmins (t ≈ −2). Step Law's rule omits it.
    - Caveat: the smoothed optima put the LR about one-third below the grid argmin (geometric-mean ratio 0.66), because the landscape is asymmetric (steep on the high-LR side); the quadratic's minimum lies above the best observed run in 16 of 17 cells, i.e. it misfits the optimum. Levels differ across methods; elasticities are more stable.
11. **(Revised in review.) Conditioning on the batch removes Step Law's positive D-elasticity of the optimal LR, but explains only a quarter to under half of the Bjorck–Step Law disagreement.** With the batch held fixed, the D-elasticity is +0.05 (0.015) smoothed and +0.02 (0.03) grid, not +0.21 to +0.31. Batch co-scaling accounts for 91% (bootstrap SE 0.15) of the unconditional elasticity with smoothed optima but only 56% (SE 0.24) with grid argmins. Conditioning closes 26% (SE 0.02) to 43% (SE 0.13) of the 0.63 gap between the published +0.307 and −0.32.
    - Caveat: our conditional +0.02 to +0.05 still differs from Bjorck's −0.32 [L]. The data cannot adjudicate this residual (0.34–0.37); it may reflect Step Law's fixed 2,000-step warmup and cosine decay to 1e-5 versus Bjorck's warmup of max(1%, 1,000 steps) and decay to 0.1× peak, or the different scale range (Bjorck: N ≥ 760M).

**Design recommendations for practitioners** (for Section 5 or the conclusion of the paper):
1. **Measure inputs fully.**
   - Count every parameter that costs FLOPs (the head), and report the convention. At sub-1B scale, the convention alone moves a by 0.12–0.17 (head) and a further 0.025–0.04 (input embeddings). The same applies to hyperparameter datasets: Step Law reports non-embedding N, and counting the head moves its frontier a by −0.02 to −0.10 (Section 1.5).
   - Proportional mis-scaling is harmless for a; scale-dependent omissions are not (Proposition M).
2. **Treat a as the fragile object.**
   - It is moved by the counting convention, by flexible inputs through the transverse inefficiency gradient, and by the unidentified E.
   - Report a from IsoFLOP designs, with its E-profile or with E-robust procedures.
   - Do not assume σ* or γ is safe instead: σ* looks stable only in natural units, and γ moves by a factor of 2.4 along the same E profile. Report every object with its E-profile and its N convention.
3. **Tune at every scale, or at least check the transverse gradient.**
   - At a few budgets, compare an untuned run with a tuned run along the IsoFLOP. If the excess loss has a slope in ln N, a is biased by about −∂_{ln C}[Δ/f''].
   - Inefficiency that depends only on compute is harmless for a.
   - With few configurations per scale, expect precision loss (Monte Carlo s.d. of a is 0.27 with 1 configuration per cell and 0.04 with 16), and with a single configuration also a downward bias in σ*.
4. **Distinguish conditional from unconditional hyperparameter laws.**
   - An LR rule fitted with the batch optimized (Step Law) must not be applied at a fixed batch, and vice versa (Bjorck).
   - Transfer between the two with the LR–batch complementarity (about 0.30–0.32 here). This removes the sign disagreement's *positive* side, not Bjorck's negative sign.
5. **Batch-size rules probably need an N term.** The smoothed optimum gives B* ∝ N^−0.20 D^0.60 (grid argmins: N^−0.36, t ≈ −2).
6. **Use the grid minimum as the concentrated technology, but model how inefficiency varies with scale.** SFA adds a variance function (inefficiency falls with D); it does not add a better frontier.

## 5. Robustness, failures and fragile results

- **Bug to report in `sl.py` (not edited).** `sl.fit_chinchilla(..., E_fixed=E)` calls L-BFGS-B with *default* tolerances. The free-E branch passes ftol = 1e-15; the E-fixed branch does not.
  - When the objective is O(1e-4) (small n, NLS or Huber), the default ftol of about 2.2e-9, which acts as an absolute tolerance when |f| < 1, stops far from the optimum.
  - Example (reproduced in review): on the Step Law NLS frontier, `sl.fit_chinchilla(..., E_fixed=1.4, init=<free-E NLS estimate>)` stops at a = 0.565 with objective (½·SSR) 1.238e-4; the optimum is a = 0.5735 with objective 1.1447e-4, which `common.fit_efixed` reaches from the same start and from DEFAULT_GRID. (The builder's example, a = 0.503, came from a different start.)
  - An earlier version of our Step Law results and of the parametric simulation check was contaminated by this. For instance, it wrongly suggested that parametric and IsoFLOP biases have opposite signs.
  - Workaround: `common.fit_efixed` uses sl's objective and gradient with the free-branch tolerances. Every E-fixed fit in this module uses it.
  - **Other modules that use `E_fixed` should check their results.**
- **Huber-LSE on 17 points is unreliable.** The Huber(δ = 1e-3) objective is essentially L1 there and has many local optima; the frontier fits give E = 0.91 under Huber and 1.43 under NLS. We use NLS for Step Law and report Huber once.
- **E is not identified in Porian's small-scale runs either.** The Huber fits to tuned runs put E at 3.13 (RW) and 2.76 (OWT2), essentially at the lowest observed losses (3.10 and 2.77; for RW, above it). So the fitted technology is a local approximation and its α, β (about 0.63 and 0.71) should not be quoted as structural.
  - Consequences: (i) the semi-synthetic measurement check and the model-based curvature in Proposition F rely on it; (ii) the E = 1.8 refits give similar measurement biases but distorted levels, e.g. a = 0.81 for tuned RW.
- **Per-budget fit of Proposition F.** The slope match is good, but per-budget predictions for steps 3 and 4 at 8e17–6e18 FLOPs are positive (+0.2 to +0.5) while observed shifts are near 0. There, δ turns negative: the cosine-decayed base runs beat the tuned constant-LR runs. The first-order formula also operates far from its validity region at the smallest budgets, where δ is as large as 1.6 nats.
- **Semi-synthetic over-prediction of the measurement bias (20–50%).** Not resolved. Candidates: a misspecified local technology, the discreteness of the architecture ladder, and head parameters being less "productive" than body parameters. The exploratory λ-profile prefers λ ≈ 0.75, but the profile is flat.
- **Step Law policy shifts** in a (up to 0.08) and σ* (up to 0.01) are within 0.70 and 0.74 residual-bootstrap SE respectively. They are illustrative only.
- **Step Law's N is non-embedding** (review). Levels of a from Step Law are in Kaplan's convention; counting the head lowers the frontier a by 0.10 (E free) or 0.02 (E = 1.4). Across-policy comparisons at fixed E are unaffected in construction, but the level results (e.g., "a = 0.563", the E-profile values) should be quoted with the convention.
- **σ* stability** (review): the natural-unit stability of σ* is not robust to normalization (Section 1.5(vii)); in SE units σ* is equally or less stable than a in every comparison.
- **Chain-rule decomposition share** (review): 91% (smoothed optima) vs 56% (grid argmins), with wide bootstrap intervals; report both.
- **Sign of the Step Law policy shifts vs linear gradients.** For the fixed-rule policies, the cell-level linear gradients of ι do not predict the sign of the parametric a-shift. Example: Porian base has ι_n − ι_d = +0.0055, which predicts a *down*-bias for an IsoFLOP design, but the parametric a rises by 0.047. The mapping from the 17-cell design to the parametric pseudo-true value is nonlinear and design-specific. Do not use the linear-gradient formula for Step Law parametric fits.
- **Random-configuration Monte Carlo with k = 1:** in at least half of the draws the E-free fit hits the E = 0 boundary (median E = 0).
- **SFA.** Half-normal σ_v → 0 is a boundary solution. Calibrating σ_v at 0.0017 leaves γ_D unchanged. The exponential model fits better (Δlog L = 122). The SFA is only as informative as the designed grid.
- **Smoothed vs grid optima.** The asymmetric landscape shifts the smoothed LR optimum about one-third below the grid argmin (geometric-mean ratio 0.66). Elasticities: N −0.69 vs −0.82; D +0.21 vs +0.29.
- **Not done / not used.**
  - The Step Law MoE file (708 runs). It has no off-path variation in D/N (sd of log D/N given C ≈ 0.002 per `data_sources.md`); a total-vs-active parameter analysis was out of scope.
  - Lourie 2026 [lourie2026small] runs are not public.
  - Porian's hyperparameter sweep runs (566) are not used beyond the published laws.
- **Load note.** The machine was heavily loaded by other modules (load average 30–115), so the builder's wall time was 33–36 minutes; the reviewer's clean reruns took 27 and 18 minutes (the latter with the de-duplicated E-fixed starts). To stay within budget, bootstrap and Monte Carlo replications use warm starts plus a 16-start grid, checked against FAST_GRID.

## 6. Open issues

0. (Review) Decide how the paper handles the σ*-stability claim in light of Section 1.5(vii): any statement must name the unit (natural, relative to α+β, or SE), and the E-profile evidence here argues against a general robustness claim.

1. Resolve the semi-synthetic over-prediction on Porian's ladder: fit the local technology with E constrained below the minimum observed loss, or use a translog. Then estimate the quality weight on head and embedding parameters properly, with a likelihood-ratio profile and bootstrap.
2. **Proposition F needs a formal statement and proof** in the theory module. The first-order statement should come with conditions (second-order remainder bounded by δ²f'''/f''²) and with the parametric (Approach 3) analogue, whose pseudo-true values depend on the design.
3. The Bjorck residual (+0.05 conditional vs −0.32) should be checked against Bjorck's own runs if they become public. A within-Step-Law check by warmup length is impossible: warmup is fixed at 2,000 steps.
4. The Chinchilla non-embedding reanalysis uses the Pearce–Song map. With Chinchilla's Table A9 architectures [hoffmann2022training] one could use exact embedding counts, which would need a digitized table.
5. Extend the conditional-vs-unconditional analysis to Porian's sweep runs (566 runs: LR × batch × β₂ at 20 tokens per parameter). That would test whether the complementarity of about 0.32 is stable.
6. `sl.py` E_fixed tolerance (Section 5): the lead author should decide whether to patch sl.py centrally.
