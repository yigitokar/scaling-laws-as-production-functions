\begin{tablenotes}
The Step Law grid of \citet{li2025predictablea}; $N$ non-embedding parameters, $D$ tokens, $B$ batch size in tokens.
Panel A: cost-type stochastic frontier $\ln L_{ij}=\mu_c+\epsilon_{ij}+\iota_{ij}$ with 17 unrestricted cell effects
$\mu_c$ (the concentrated technology), noise $\epsilon\sim N(0,\sigma_v^2)$ and inefficiency $\iota\ge0$ with
$\ln\sigma_u=\gamma_0+\gamma_N(\ln N-\overline{\ln N})+\gamma_D(\ln D-\overline{\ln D})$ \citep{caudill1995frontier}, on
the non-diverged runs (loss at most 35 percent above the cell minimum); numerical-Hessian standard errors in
parentheses; cell-clustered sandwich standard errors of $(\gamma_N,\gamma_D)$ are (0.076, 0.032) for half-normal and
(0.070, 0.023) for exponential inefficiency. Fixing $\sigma_v$ at the median within-cell
residual standard deviation (0.0017) gives $\gamma_D$ of $-0.229$ and $-0.262$. Because the grid is designed, its
minimum measures the frontier directly: the estimated frontier coincides with the best run in each cell (mean absolute
gap at most 0.11 percent), and the stochastic frontier adds only the variance function. Last row: OLS across cells of
the log mean inefficiency (HC1 standard errors). Panel B: optimal learning rate in each cell regressed on $\ln N$,
$\ln D$ and, holding the batch fixed, $\ln B$. Grid argmin: best run in the cell; smoothed: minimum of a quadratic in
$(\ln\eta,\ln B)$ fitted to runs within 2 percent of the cell minimum. Standard errors: two-level bootstrap (cells, then
each cell's optimum; 1,000 draws) for unconditional demands, cell-clustered for conditional ones; Step Law's own
bootstrap for its rule; the rule of \citet{bjorck2024scaling} holds the batch fixed. With the batch fixed at 0.52 million tokens the elasticities are $-0.495$ (0.077) and 0.040
(0.047). The batch-size demand has a $D$-elasticity of 0.598 (0.021), smoothed, against Step Law's 0.571. Panel C:
the unconditional elasticity equals the conditional one plus the learning-rate--batch complementarity times the
batch elasticity \citep{milgrom1996lechatelier}; the published gap is between Step Law's $+0.307$ and Bj\"orck et al.'s
$-0.32$ for models of at least 760 million parameters. Against their steeper per-size exponents for small models (down
to $-0.70$) the shares closed would be about 0.16 and 0.27. Cell-cluster bootstrap standard errors (1,000 draws).
\end{tablenotes}
