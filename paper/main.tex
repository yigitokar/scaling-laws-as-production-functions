\begin{abstract}
Language-model developers trade off quality, training cost and the value of compactness. Optimization cuts both ways:
it removes the variation identifying the technology, as curvature information is fourth order in allocation errors,
but makes choices informative about objectives. Annealed designed experiments give, without functional-form
restrictions, a parameter-data elasticity of substitution near 0.7 at $10^{19}$--$3\times10^{20}$ FLOP and about 0.6
at two designs' largest budgets. \textcolor{red}{[TBD-m9 (Amendments~1--2, Q1): outcomes~1--2 add ``A controlled
experiment at $2\times10^{14}$--$10^{17}$ FLOP gives [x, y] for two corpora'' with the cuts of M9-14; outcome~3 replaces
the $\sigma^*$ clause by the range across designs, compute levels and the experiment; outcome~4: no change.]}
Inverted first-order conditions show that most dense open-weight models with disclosed training data released since
2023 were trained beyond training-cost minimization, though not most training compute, and that over-training rose
steeply from 2023 to 2025.
\end{abstract}
