\begin{tablenotes}The 240-run Chinchilla sample. IsoFLOP minima: a parabola in $\ln N$ fitted to the 132
IsoFLOP-profile runs of each of the nine budgets, with the argmins regressed on $\ln(C/6)$ (conditional factor demand,
Hoffmann et al.'s Approach 2) and $L^*_b=E+K(C_b/6)^{-\gamma}$ fitted to the nine minima (the IsoFLOP-minima frontier,
an inverse cost function), with $E$ free or fixed at the primal value. This frontier is not Hoffmann et al.'s
Approach 1, which uses envelopes of training curves that the digitization does not contain and which also reports
allocation exponents. Approach 3 is the Huber primal on all 240 runs. Hoffmann and Besiroglu denote the published
parameters of \citet{hoffmann2022training} and \citet{besiroglu2024chinchilla}. ``Analytic'' uses the closed forms
$a=\beta/(\alpha+\beta)$, $\gamma=\alpha\beta/(\alpha+\beta)$, $N^*=G(C/6)^a$ and $L^*=E+K(C/6)^{-\gamma}$; ``design''
(design-consistent) applies the IsoFLOP-minima procedure to the primal's fitted values at the observed design, which
removes the finite-grid bias of parabola fits \citep{czech2026problems}. $\bar C$ is the geometric mean of the budgets.
Standard errors in parentheses: within-budget stratified pairs bootstrap (300 draws). Panel B: Wald statistics with an
outlier-robust bootstrap covariance and $\chi^2_2$ $p$-values; calibrated $p$-values take critical values from the
bootstrap distribution of the same statistic (stratified pairs / fixed-design wild bootstrap); the classical test
regresses the nine argmin gaps on $[1,\ln C_b]$ and treats the null technology as known. For published parameters only
the IsoFLOP-minima side is random. With all 245 runs, the design-consistent path test of our refit gives a calibrated
$p$ of 0.054 (stratified) and a classical $p$ of 0.041.\end{tablenotes}
