\begin{tablenotes}
Compute needed to reach the loss of the compute-optimal model when training at $k$ times the compute-optimal
tokens-per-parameter ratio, relative to the minimum, in the Chinchilla family:
$C/C_{\min}=((\alpha+\beta w)/(\alpha+\beta))^{1/\gamma}w^{-1/\alpha}$ with $\ln w=(1/\sigma^*-1)\ln k$. Entries are for
the homothetic case $\alpha=\beta=\rho=1/\sigma^*-1$, where $C/C_{\min}=\cosh(\rho\ln k/2)^{2/\rho}$; every entry was
checked by direct minimization. At the same $\sigma^*$, the $\alpha/\beta$ split of \citet{besiroglu2024chinchilla}
changes the entries by at most 4.5 percent and that of \citet{hoffmann2022training} by at most 19 percent for $k\le100$
($k$ relative to $M^*$ at the model's own compute). $s=(w-1)/w$ is the expenditure share of the value of compactness
that rationalizes over-training by $k$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:wedge}); for a developer that serves its model it is the
planned serving share of lifetime cost. With the parameters of \citet{devries2023go} ($\alpha=0.32$, $\beta=0.28$,
$\sigma^*=0.77$), a model at 30 percent of the compute-optimal size needs 6.6 times the tokens and 99 percent more
compute ($k=21.1$); the formula gives 99 percent.
