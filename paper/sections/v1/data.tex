\section{Data}\label{sec:data}

We use three kinds of data, which correspond to the distinction in production-function estimation between
experimental and observational variation in inputs (Table~\ref{tab:data}). In designed sweeps the experimenter
chooses parameters and tokens, including runs far from the compute-optimal path. Our own sweep is a controlled
experiment with two ``labs'' that differ only in their training data. Released models are observational: labs chose
their inputs, and in Section~\ref{sec:wedge} those choices are the object of study. Throughout, $N$ counts
parameters, $D$ counts tokens processed (including repetitions), $C=6ND$ and $M=D/N$. Online Appendix B documents
the construction of every sample.

For identification, the key column of Table~\ref{tab:data} is the residual standard deviation of a regression of
$\ln M$ on $\ln C$, $\mathrm{sd}(\ln M\,|\,\ln C)$, which we call the off-path spread. A sample of compute-optimal runs
has a spread of zero. Under the Chinchilla form, $\ln w=[(\alpha+\beta)/2]\ln(M/M^{*}(C))$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:wedge}), and $\ln M^{*}(C)$ is linear in $\ln C$. The off-path spread times $(\alpha+\beta)/2$ is
therefore the transverse spread of Section~\ref{sec:ident}, the dispersion of log wedges around the best-fitting path.
By Proposition~\ref{prop:info}, information about $M^{*}$ grows with the square of that dispersion and information
about $\sigma^{*}$ with its fourth power. On the Chinchilla extraction the off-path spread is 1.35, which corresponds
to $\mathrm{sd}(\ln w)=0.48$.

\subsection{Designed Sweeps}

The canonical data are the 245 runs that \citet{besiroglu2024chinchilla} digitized from \citet{hoffmann2022training}.
We follow them in dropping the five highest-loss runs, which leaves 240. In these data $D=C/(6N)$ is constructed rather
than observed, and the digitized loss is quantized to about $\pm0.01$. For cluster-robust inference we assign each run
to one of the nine nominal IsoFLOP budgets.

Five further sweeps report run-level losses. Farseer \citep{li2025predictableb} is a near-factorial grid of 404 runs
with one data recipe and $M$ from 0.31 to 2,570. Its off-path spread of 1.97 is the largest among public sweeps, tied
with that of the 33 single-epoch runs of \citet{muennighoff2023scaling}. \citet{gadre2024language} train 104 models on
three web corpora and evaluate all of them on the same C4 validation set, which provides a common output for testing
whether better data is factor-neutral. The OLMo ladder \citep{bhagia2024establishing} adds 30 runs. DataDecide
\citep{magnusson2025datadecide} crosses 25 data recipes with 14 model sizes, but its final checkpoints all have
$D\approx100N$. Only intermediate checkpoints, evaluated before the learning-rate schedule has finished, break the
collinearity, so we use DataDecide only to compare recipes. Two further IsoFLOP designs complete the set: Meta's Llama~3
profiles \citep{grattafiori2024llama}, as digitized by \citet{czech2026llama3isoflop}, and Marin's profiles on three
corpora \citep{marin2026ladders}. Finally, \citet{porian2024resolving} (975 runs under four FLOP-counting conventions)
and Step Law \citep{li2025predictablea} (1,911 runs on learning-rate and batch-size grids in 17 $(N,D)$ cells) vary
how inputs are counted and tuned; Section~\ref{sec:tech:measurement} uses them to study input measurement and flexible
inputs.

The sweeps disagree on conventions. Some count all parameters and others exclude embeddings, tokenizers differ, and one
sweep reports bits per character where the others report nats per token. We therefore compare across sweeps only
unit-free objects ($\alpha$, $\beta$, $a$, $\gamma$, $\sigma$, $\kappa$), never $E$, $A$, $B$ or $M^{*}$. The designs
differ too: the off-path spread ranges from 0.73 in DataDecide to 1.97, and Online Appendix
Figure~\ref{fig:app_designs} plots each design against its isocost lines.

\subsection{A Controlled Two-Lab Experiment}

The public sweeps differ in tokenizer, parameter count, output and recipe all at once, and none varies data quality
alone on a factorial design that reaches the token-to-parameter ratios of released small models. (For 55 percent of
the open-weight models in Section~\ref{sec:wedge}, $M$ exceeds the Chinchilla design's maximum of 341.) We therefore
ran our own experiment. The two ``labs'' train on FineWeb-Edu and on FineWeb (sample-10BT subsets;
\citealp{penedo2024fineweb}). FineWeb-Edu keeps the FineWeb documents that an educational-quality classifier rates
highly, so the labs draw on the same English web crawl and differ in how selectively it is filtered. Both use one
byte-level BPE tokenizer with 8,192 tokens, trained on an equal mix of the corpora, so losses are in the same units.

The design crosses eight model sizes with six token budgets. The models are decoder-only transformers of width
$d\in\{128,\dots,640\}$, depth $d/64$ and head dimension 64, with 0.39M--49M non-embedding parameters (1.4M--54M
including the tied embedding). Each is trained to $D\in\{25,50,100,200,400,800\}$M tokens, with budgets capped at
400M for widths 448 and 512 and at 200M for width 640. This gives 44 endpoints per lab, with $M$ from 0.51 to 2,031 and
an off-path spread of 1.96, as large as Farseer's; no run repeats data (Online Appendix
Table~\ref{tab:app_sweepdesign}).

Training uses constant learning rates with cooldown branches \citep{hagele2024scaling}. Each width has one
constant-learning-rate trunk; for each budget $D_k$, a copy of the trunk branches off at $0.8D_k$ and its learning rate
decays to zero over the remaining 20 percent. Every endpoint is thus a fully annealed
run, which avoids the unfinished-schedule problem of checkpoint data. We use AdamW \citep{loshchilov2019decoupled}
with moment decay rates 0.9 and 0.99, weight decay 0.1, batches of $64\times256$ tokens and a peak learning rate
$\mathrm{lr}^{*}(d)=3.07\times10^{-3}(d/256)^{-0.90}$ fitted to a calibration sweep. Within a lab, all widths share
the initialization seed and the data order (common random numbers); seed replicates at three widths measure the noise
from initialization and data order. All models were trained on a single Apple M5 Max \citep{hannun2023mlx}.

Every endpoint is evaluated on 1,048,576 held-out tokens of \emph{both} corpora, so the two labs' models share a common
output. A test of factor-neutral data quality requires one; it is the analogue of measuring output in physical units
rather than in revenue. Section~\ref{sec:tech:experiment} reports the results. The design has two limitations. Its
largest runs use under $10^{17}$ FLOP, five orders of magnitude less than the largest Chinchilla runs. And the learning
rate is tuned to width but not to $D$ or to the lab, so any remaining hyperparameter inefficiency is a flexible-input
bias of the kind studied in Section~\ref{sec:tech:measurement}.

\subsection{Observational Data}

Section~\ref{sec:wedge} uses two samples of released models. Sample~B contains 173 general-purpose open-weight base
models released in 2021--2025. Parameter counts are exact counts of the checkpoint tensors on the Hugging Face Hub where
available, with active parameters for mixture-of-experts models. Token counts come from \citet{ruan2024observational},
model cards and technical reports, cross-checked against Epoch AI. The production-scale universe adds closed and less
documented models from the Epoch AI database \citep{epochai2026data}: 335 models trained with $6ND\ge10^{21}$ FLOP in
2019--2026, of which 271 are open weight. Usage outcomes (Hugging Face downloads, OpenRouter listings, LMArena votes)
come from snapshots taken in September 2026. Section~\ref{sec:obs} uses a benchmark panel of 128 dense base models from
38 families, drawn from \citet{ruan2024observational} and \citet{maiapolo2024sloth}, whose outputs are log odds of
above-chance accuracy on HellaSwag, ARC-Challenge and Winogrande. It also uses the 231 observations from 144 papers
compiled by \citet{ho2024algorithmic}, whose $D$ is dataset size rather than tokens processed and whose output is log
perplexity, mostly per word.

These data do not lack off-path variation: the benchmark panel's spread is 1.64, above Chinchilla's. They lack
exogeneity, because labs chose the variation for reasons that may be correlated with productivity. Measurement is a
smaller problem. Token counts are disclosed rather than audited, and models with undisclosed counts, such as Mistral~7B
and Mixtral, drop out. Under the reference technology a 10 percent error in $D$ moves the revealed wedge by about 4
percent, because $\partial\ln w/\partial\ln D=\beta\approx0.37$.
