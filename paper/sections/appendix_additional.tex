\section[The Technology: Additional Results]{Appendix \thesection. The Technology: Additional Results}\label{app:additional}
\setlength{\emergencystretch}{2em}% allow looser lines in the online appendix

This appendix supports Sections~\ref{sec:framework} through~\ref{sec:tech}: a dictionary between scaling-law practice and
production economics; estimators and inference on the Chinchilla data; the model-free estimator, its budget-level
estimates and their drift with compute; specification tests across sweeps; the compute cost of over-training; and input
measurement and flexible inputs.

\subsection{A Dictionary}\label{app:add-dictionary}

Table~\ref{tab:app-dictionary} lists nineteen correspondences. Three analogies break down: parameters and tokens have no
market prices, so size-dependent prices of training and serving FLOPs enter the first-order conditions as wedges; returns
to scale measured in loss depend on how output is cardinalized, whereas the elasticity of substitution, the optimal mix
and the wedge do not; and the inputs of a designed sweep are assigned, not chosen, so the endogeneity machinery of IO

\subsection{The Chinchilla Data}\label{app:add-chinchilla}

\textit{Estimators and inference.} On the 240-run sample (Online Appendix~\ref{app:chinchilla}; total parameters), Huber loss with
$\delta=10^{-3}$, the estimator of \citet{besiroglu2024chinchilla}, is least absolute deviations in practice. Least squares,
on log loss or in levels, gives a larger data exponent (paired differences in $\beta$ of 0.039 and 0.060) and
compute-optimal ratios at $5.76\times10^{23}$ FLOP lower by 3.7 and 7.6 tokens per parameter. Estimating the loss equation
jointly with the nine IsoFLOP argmins under the restrictions of Lemma~\ref{lem:alloc} leaves the estimates unchanged
($p=0.60$). The 41 runs within 0.15 log points of the expansion path carry less than 1 percent of their input variance
across the path, and the condition number of the Jacobian rises from 62 on the full design to 413 on them, against 69 to 95
for random subsamples of the same size. The restriction $\kappa=1$ is rejected at 1 percent under every bootstrap scheme
except nine budget clusters that assign each off-profile run to the nearest budget ($p=0.052$); clustering off-profile runs
by their model-size trunk gives $p=0.004$ and a 95 percent interval for $M^*(5.76\times10^{23})$ of $[5.2,106]$. The CES
restriction $\alpha=\beta$ is not rejected at 5 percent by tests robust to the heavy tails of the residuals
(restricted-bootstrap $p=0.17$ for the Gaussian likelihood ratio; heteroskedasticity-robust Wald $p=0.06$--0.09). With
$\kappa$ free, the Huber profile over $\sigma^*$ has its minimum at 0.701, with a bootstrap-calibrated 95 percent set of
$[0.68,0.72]$; on the 41 on-path runs the profile is flat (95 percent set $[0.20,0.99]$ with $\chi^2_1$ critical values,
the edge of the grid; about $[0.15,0.99]$ with bootstrap critical values). The
Chinchilla-70B wedge contains one under every scheme, an internal-consistency check with little power because DeepMind
sized that model on these runs.

\textit{Exclusion rules, duality and lab designs.} As the $j$ highest-loss runs are dropped, $\beta$ falls from 0.453
with all 245 runs to 0.347 at $j=15$, while $\alpha$ stays between 0.345 and 0.349; the five runs that Besiroglu et al.\ drop
are data-starved runs whose influence is leverage, not truncation bias (a truncated-Gaussian likelihood,
\citealp{hausman1977social}, returns the same $\beta$). The Chinchilla-70B wedge is 1.54 with them and 0.95 to 1.06 under
ten of twelve rules that remove all or most of them (the $j$ highest-loss runs, the largest residuals, or runs with $D/N$
below 0.4, 1 or 2); dropping the whole $10^{19}$ budget gives 0.78, and the 132 IsoFLOP-profile runs without them give
1.16. Against design-consistent nulls, which allow for the finite-grid bias of fitted parabolas
\citep{czech2026problems}, our refit is consistent with the IsoFLOP argmins ($p\ge0.16$). Our argmin fit reproduces Meta's
Llama~3 law; on its eight bracketed budgets the allocation exponent is 0.50 (a path slope $e_p$ near zero) and $M^*(10^{24})$ is 15
to 16 instead of 31. For the Marin
ladders the design-consistent tests do not reject ($p$ between 0.33 and 0.54).

\subsection{The Model-Free Estimator and the Elasticity by Compute}\label{app:add-modelfree}

\textit{Specification.} The primary estimator of Section~\ref{sec:tech} fits a quadratic in $\ln N$ to the runs within one
log point of a pooled IsoFLOP-argmin path, on budgets whose minimum is bracketed by at least two runs on each side, and
pools budgets by a random-effects mean. By Remark~\ref{rem:A-grid}, the noise-free finite-grid bias of such a quadratic is
about $-0.003$ in $\sigma^*$ on a grid spanning a factor of 9 in $N$ and below 0.001 on grids narrower than a factor of
about 3; the primary window spans a factor of $e^2\approx7.4$, and on each design's own fitted technologies the bias is
at most 0.006. In the specification checks, pooled $F$ tests of a cubic term never reject at 5 percent, and across
windows, polynomial orders, frontier smoothers and window centering the random-effects $\sigma^*$ ranges over
0.656--0.702 for Chinchilla (0.665--0.714 in total parameters; 0.58 with the narrowest window, which keeps seven of nine
budgets), 0.642--0.677 for Llama~3 and 0.66--0.72 for Marin (0.64 with DCLM's narrowest window, which keeps four
budgets); an independent re-implementation, with Chinchilla in total parameters, gives 0.669--0.694. In a Monte Carlo at
the fitted $\kappa$-family technologies of Chinchilla (in total parameters), Llama~3 and Marin's DCLM ladder, the
random-effects interval covers the truth in 93 to 95 percent of 500 replications with a bias of at most 0.006, and in
93 to 94 percent when half the noise variance is shared within a budget. It covers 90 to 97 percent also when the
shared half attaches to model size across budgets or to a smooth distortion in size, but such a distortion, which a
digitized figure can produce, makes the within-design drift test reject a true zero drift 14 to 19 percent of the time
(200 replications). On a $\kappa$-family fit to Porian et al.'s runs the noise-free bias is about $+0.05$. The estimator needs exact isocosts, so each design is analyzed in FLOP-effective parameters, the
convention in which its profiles are exact isocosts. On Marin, whose accounting is observed, the configuration count
gives 0.04 to 0.07 less.

\textit{FLOP accounting.} If FLOPs per token of the measured parameter count vary with size with elasticity $\eta$, a
design's $\sigma^*$ shifts by about $0.44\eta$. Meta's accounting is unobserved, and $\eta=\pm0.1$ for Meta moves the
study-level mean to 0.673 or 0.700; in Marin, counting configuration parameters lowers the mean to 0.673, and counting
Farseer's embeddings lowers it to 0.670. For Chinchilla, FLOPs per token rebuilt from Hoffmann et al.'s architecture
table (Online Appendix~\ref{app:chinchilla}) have an elasticity with respect to $N$ between $-0.04$ and 0.00 by budget,
and Chinchilla's $\sigma^*$ is 0.660 against 0.673 in total parameters (Table~\ref{tab:app-chinflop}). It is
0.648--0.660 across Hoffmann et al.'s printed, implemented and executed counts on the nominal budgets, and 0.690 in
non-embedding parameters; moved onto each count's own exact isocosts, every count and convention gives 0.660--0.669. Of
Hoffmann et al.'s counts, only the rebuilt one reproduces their Table A4 and fits the FLOP coordinates of their
Figure~4. On the 137 profile runs the rebuilt count gives less than the total count in every specification of the grid,
by 0.008 to 0.018 in the eleven specifications that use all nine budgets and by 0.022 with the narrowest window.
At the primary specification about a third of the difference comes from one run that enters the $3\times10^{19}$ window.
The level is less robust than the direction. The five high-loss runs lie outside every window but move the window
centers: without them the primary specification gives 0.681 (0.674 in total parameters). With profile membership drawn
on the corrected coordinates it gives 0.668, and with $N_F$ read directly off the digitized coordinates, as for
Llama~3, 0.676. Put in place of 0.660, these values give study-level means of 0.691 to 0.694
(Table~\ref{tab:app-chinflop-prop}), and Cochran's statistic across studies falls from 8.3 ($p=0.04$) to 5.8--6.3 ($p$
between 0.10 and 0.12). Across all of Chinchilla's windows, samples and memberships the study-level mean runs from 0.674
to 0.702 and $Q$ from 4.4 to 14.8, so we build no claim on the heterogeneity across studies. The reference technology of Section~\ref{sec:wedge} stays in total parameters; refitted
in $N_F$, its $\sigma^*$ is 0.700 with $\kappa$ free, against 0.701.

\textit{Budget-level estimates.} Tables~\ref{tab:app-budgets} and~\ref{tab:app-budgets-b} list every bracketed budget with its runs on each side of
the minimum. Fixed-effect and random-effects pooling agree on Marin (0.70--0.71) and nearly so on Chinchilla (0.666 against
0.660), but not on Llama~3 (0.628 against 0.660), whose precise large budgets pull the fixed-effect mean down. Cochran's $Q$ rejects homogeneity within Llama~3 ($p=0.001$), but
not within Chinchilla ($Q=12.7$, bootstrap $p=0.06$) or Marin's corpora.

\textit{Drift with compute.} Table~\ref{tab:app-sigmaC} pools the 44 budget-level estimates of six designs (Farseer's local
path counts as a sixth design) in a three-level random-effects meta-regression on $\log_{10}C$
\citep{konstantopoulos2011fixed}. The slope is $-0.033$ per decade. With Marin's three corpora as one cluster there are
four clusters, and six with the corpora apart, so the cluster-robust (CR2) standard errors
\citep{bell2002bias,pustejovsky2018small} have about three degrees of freedom and a wild cluster bootstrap-$t$ with
Webb weights has only $6^4=1{,}296$ distinct draws with four clusters; we enumerate them. In the primary row with Marin
as one cluster the two tests disagree (CR2 $p=0.11$, wild $p=0.049$), and across weighting, scale, test, cluster
definition and design fixed effects the $p$-value lies between 0.03 and 0.49. The design fixed-effects rows, which use
only variation within designs, give $-0.021$ (inverse-variance weights; CR2 $p=0.49$, wild $p=0.37$) and $-0.026$
(unweighted; 0.17 and 0.051 with Marin as one cluster, 0.065 and 0.073 with six).

The drift comes from the budgets above $3\times10^{20}$ FLOP, which only Chinchilla and Llama~3 reach. Across all 17 of
their budgets $\sigma^*$ falls by 0.058 per decade, with a standard error of 0.014 from the joint bootstrap draws of the
budget-level estimates, which share each design's frontier derivative, scaled for the budgets' excess dispersion (0.009
from sampling error alone; model-based 0.013). A hinge at $3\times10^{20}$ gives $-0.152$ above the knot. At or below
$3\times10^{20}$ FLOP the pooled slope is $-0.008$ ($p$ between 0.56 and 0.87 in the primary row), but the designs' own
slopes differ there (Wald 13.9, $p=0.016$), and Llama~3's is $-0.050$ (s.e.\ 0.017). Leaving out each design's largest
budget gives $-0.030$ ($p$ between 0.19 and 0.29). The five budgets at $6\times10^{20}$ FLOP and above have an
inverse-variance mean of 0.594, 85 percent of it from Llama~3 (46 percent at $6\times10^{20}$ and 38 percent at
$10^{21}$); with the variance of the joint draws, its modified Hartung--Knapp--Sidik--Jonkman interval is [0.561, 0.627].
Without Chinchilla's five highest-loss runs, which move its $6\times10^{20}$ estimate from 0.550 to 0.665, the mean is
0.598. Farseer's local path, which reaches $10^{21}$ FLOP without bracketed budgets, gives 0.722 (0.008) at
$5\times10^{20}$ and 0.658 (0.011) at $10^{21}$; with the latter the six top estimates are heterogeneous ($Q=21.1$,
$p<0.001$; random-effects mean 0.606, fixed-effect mean 0.625). With Marin as one cluster the pooled line gives 0.680 at
$10^{20}$ FLOP, with a CR2 interval of [0.638, 0.722]. At $10^{19}$ the CR2 standard error is an eighth of the
model-based one, a small-sample artifact, so we report only the point estimate there, 0.713.

In a Monte Carlo on the grids of Chinchilla (in total parameters), Llama~3 and Marin's DCLM ladder with a known path of
$\sigma^*(C)$, the estimator creates no drift when there is none and attenuates true drifts by at most 20 percent, but a
single design has little power against $-0.06$ per decade: 0.31 on Marin's grid and 0.37 on Chinchilla's, against 0.72
on Llama~3's. Count-symmetric windows and a power-law frontier with $E$ fitted leave the Chinchilla and Llama~3 drift at
$-0.049$ to $-0.064$, and Porian et al.'s unannealed profiles drift the other way ($+0.040$), so the estimator does not
force a negative slope.

\textit{The level.} One estimate per study (Marin one study) gives a mean of 0.687 with a modified
Hartung--Knapp--Sidik--Jonkman interval of $[0.640,0.734]$. Restricted to budgets up to $3\times10^{20}$ FLOP, it is
0.708, with an interval of [0.690, 0.726] in which Farseer's first-derivative estimate carries 84 percent of the weight.
With Farseer's bandwidth range treated as uncertainty, the restricted mean is 0.701, with an interval of [0.665, 0.738],
and the mean over all budgets 0.680. Across FLOP accounting, conventions, Chinchilla's windows, samples and memberships,
estimators and Farseer's bandwidths the mean ranges over 0.663--0.702, and the lowest interval end is 0.596
(Table~\ref{tab:app-sigma-study}).

\textit{Farseer's local expansion path.} Farseer's grid has no IsoFLOP profiles, so we estimate the loss surface locally: a
Gaussian product-kernel local quadratic of $\ln L$ in $(\ln N,\ln D)$, with cross-validated bandwidths, gives the local
wedge $w=(L_c-L_x)/(L_c+L_x)$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:A-modelfree}(i) everywhere, and the point with $w=1$ lies inside the
design on eight isocosts from $5\times10^{18}$ to $10^{21}$ FLOP. The elasticity there, from the local Hessian, has a
random-effects mean of 0.703 (simple mean 0.707) and moves between 0.66 and 0.73 with the bandwidth. A first-derivative estimator is more robust: by
Proposition~\ref{prop:A-modelfree}(iii) the slope of $\ln w$ in $u=\ln(M/M^*(C))$ at $u=0$ equals $1/\sigma^*-1$, and a
regression of the local $\ln w$ on $u$ and $u^2$ gives $\sigma^*=0.708$, between 0.701 and 0.711 under every bandwidth and
0.670 with embedding parameters counted. The quadratic coefficient, 0.020 (s.e.\ 0.001), shows that $\ln w$ is convex in $u$,
which no member of the $\kappa$ family allows. Porian et al.'s unannealed profiles give $\sigma^*$ near 0.51 and are
window-sensitive (0.45 to 0.55 across windows of 0.8 to 1.5 log points; 0.33--0.34 with a global quadratic); the gap between an unannealed and an annealed loss plausibly varies with $D/N$ at fixed
compute, so we keep them out of the range reported in Section~\ref{sec:tech}.

\subsection{The Technology across Sweeps}\label{app:add-tech}

Across the seven sweep--corpus technologies from five studies, the restriction $\kappa=1$ is rejected in every sweep
(Wald $p<0.001$; $\hat\kappa$ from 0.22 on the OLMo ladder to 0.77 on Chinchilla). CES is not rejected by the Huber-based
Wald test in any sweep ($p$ between 0.22 and 0.90), and rank-one curvature, which the $\kappa$ family imposes, is rejected
in one to two sweeps depending on the estimate of $E$. Table~\ref{tab:app-kappa-robust} reports $\sigma^*_\kappa$ under
changes of sample, convention and irreducible loss. On Farseer it is stable across size exclusions (0.694 to 0.716) except
for the parameter-count convention (0.668 with both embedding matrices counted). On Chinchilla it falls to 0.690 and 0.648
when the smallest tenth and quarter of the runs are dropped. The small sweeps are fragile: on Gadre et al.'s corpora it moves
between 0.48 and 0.70 when one or two of the six sizes are dropped, so the heterogeneity of $\sigma^*_\kappa$ across sweeps
may partly reflect how the $\kappa$ family fits small designs without IsoFLOP profiles. In the Chinchilla form, conventions
matter most for the allocation exponent: counting Farseer's two untied embedding matrices moves $a$ from 0.526 to 0.411.
With task bits per byte instead of C4 cross-entropy, the OLMo ladder's $\sigma^*_\kappa$ falls from 0.544 to 0.512: two
outputs can have different isoquants (Online Appendix~\ref{app:wedge-output}).

\textit{Neutrality of data quality.} What matters for allocation and for the wedge is the tilt $\ln(A_r/B_r)$ between
recipes, which is ordinal; asymptote shifts and neutral scalings of reducible loss leave the isoquant map unchanged. On a
common C4 validation output, across the three web corpora of \citet{gadre2024language} a Hicks-neutral scaling with
corpus-specific asymptotes is not rejected ($p=0.90$), but neither are the factor-augmenting alternatives. Across the 25
DataDecide recipes \citep{magnusson2025datadecide} every restriction is rejected at the resolution of the bootstrap
($p$ at its floor with 19 or 99 draws); asymptote shifts alone explain 98.1 percent of the cross-recipe variation in fit,
and the remaining tilt, 0.22 to 0.26, implies compute-optimal ratios that differ by a factor of 2.9 to 3.4 and moves
revealed wedges by a factor of 1.25 to 1.29 at the same inputs. DataDecide's losses are taken before the learning-rate
schedule ends (Online Appendix~\ref{app:data}), and on a common validation set part of any corpus difference is
distribution match. On final checkpoints only, one ray at $M$ of about 100, the recipes' tilt is not identified: the fit
has two optima of almost equal fit that exchange the roles of the two power terms and reverse the ranking of the
recipes' tilts, so we do not report a final-checkpoint allowance. The sign result's breakdown frontier (Online Appendix
Figure~\ref{fig:app-breakdown}) does not depend on DataDecide. \citet{bi2024deepseek} report that higher-quality data
shift the compute-optimal allocation toward model size.

\subsection{The Compute Cost of Over-Training}\label{app:add-overhead}

Practitioners use the elasticity of substitution through one quantity: the extra training compute needed to reach the
loss of the compute-optimal model while training at $k$ times the compute-optimal ratio $M^*$. Table~\ref{tab:app-overhead}
reports it. Over-training by a factor of 10 costs 39 percent ($\sigma^*=0.8$) to 124 percent ($\sigma^*=0.6$) more
compute; by a factor of 100 it costs 3.5 to 14 times the minimum, and by 1,000 it costs 14 to 129 times. At $k=100$,
$\sigma^*=0.70$ implies 7.2 times the minimum compute and $\sigma^*=0.74$, the value that the Chinchilla form imposes on
the Chinchilla data, 5.4 times. The entries assume a homothetic split of the exponents; the splits of
\citet{besiroglu2024chinchilla} and \citet{hoffmann2022training} change them by at most 4.5 and 19 percent for $k\le100$.
The compute-overhead curve of \citet{devries2023go} lies between our curves for $\sigma^*=0.74$ and 0.80. The implied
wedge $w=k^{1/\sigma^*-1}$ links the table to Section~\ref{sec:wedge}: a developer that trains 100 times beyond $M^*$
reveals a share $s=(w-1)/w$ of 0.68 to 0.95, depending on $\sigma^*$.

\subsection{Measurement and Flexible Inputs}\label{app:add-measurement}

\input{tables/appD_tuning}

The largest disagreement in the scaling literature concerns the allocation exponent: $N^*\propto C^{0.73}$ in
\citet{kaplan2020scaling} against about 0.5 in \citet{hoffmann2022training}. Counting the parameters that cost FLOPs is input
measurement \citep{collardwexler2016production}, and warmup, schedule, learning rate and batch size are flexible inputs,
which are not concentrated out of the production function unless re-optimized at each scale
\citep{ackerberg2015identification,gandhi2020identification}. On the 975 released runs of \citet{porian2024resolving}, whose
ten published exponents we reproduce to within 0.003, the exponent falls from 0.837 to 0.496 on RefinedWeb; counting the
head accounts for 39 percent of the drop (47 on OpenWebText2) and flexible inputs for the rest.

\textit{Input measurement.}\label{app:add-biasformulas} Let the true technology be $L=E+AN^{-\alpha}+BD^{-\beta}$ and let
the researcher count $N_m$ parameters when the true count is $N=N_m+X(N_m)$, with omitted share $q\equiv X/N$ and a constant
scale elasticity $\theta_X\equiv d\ln X/d\ln N_m<1$. Taking the IsoFLOP argmin in measured units, with $C_m=6N_mD$,
$\vartheta\equiv d\ln N/dn_m=1-q(1-\theta_X)$ and $\zeta\equiv d\ln\vartheta/dn_m$, the local measured allocation exponent is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-measbias}
a_m=\frac{\beta}{\beta+\alpha\vartheta-\zeta},\qquad
\zeta=\frac{(1-\theta_X)\,q\,(\vartheta-\theta_X)}{\vartheta}\ge0 .
\end{equation}
Since $\vartheta\le1$ and $\zeta\ge0$, $a_m\ge a$, with equality when $q=0$ or $\theta_X=1$: only the part of the error
that is correlated with scale biases the exponent, and the bias vanishes at scale as the omitted share falls, the
functional-form lesson of \citet{nerlove1963returns}. As $q\to1$, $a_m\to\beta/(\beta+\alpha\theta_X)$, the small-scale limit
of \citet{pearce2024reconciling}; with their embedding map ($\theta_X=1/3$) the formula predicts 0.792 over Kaplan et al.'s
size range under Besiroglu et al.'s parameters, against the 0.78 they obtain by simulation.

\textit{Flexible inputs.}\label{app:add-flexible} Let observed loss be $L_{\rm obs}=L^*e^{\iota(N,D)}$, where $L^*$ is the
technology with the hyperparameters concentrated out and $\iota\ge0$ is the inefficiency of the researcher's policy. Along
the IsoFLOP curve at compute $C$ the observed argmin solves $f'(n)+\Delta(n)=0$, with $f$ the true profile, $f''=(\alpha+\beta)\gamma R^*$
at the minimizer and $\Delta\equiv\partial(L_{\rm obs}-L^*)/\partial n$ at fixed compute. To first order the log argmin
shifts by $-\Delta(C)/f''(C)$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:app-flexbias}
a_{\rm obs}-a=-\frac{\partial\,[\Delta/f'']}{\partial\ln C},
\end{equation}
with $\Delta=L^*e^{\iota}(\iota_n-\iota_d)$ at the minimizer. Inefficiency that depends on the inputs only through compute
($\iota_n=\iota_d$) leaves $a$ unbiased; with small constant gradients,
$a_{\rm obs}-a=-(\iota_n-\iota_d)E/[(\alpha+\beta)R^*(C)]$, first order in the gradient although the effect on the loss level
is second order. On Porian et al.'s runs it explains 47 to 114 percent of the three flexible-input steps.

On the Step Law grid of \citet{li2025predictablea}, inefficiency falls with training tokens (elasticity $-0.23$ to $-0.26$) but not detectably with parameters,
and one random configuration per cell biases $\sigma^*$ by $-0.051$. Holding the batch fixed shrinks the token elasticity of
the optimal learning rate to 0.02--0.05 \citep[the conditional demand of][]{milgrom1996lechatelier}, which closes 26 to 43
percent of the gap to the estimate of $-0.32$ of \citet{bjorck2024scaling}, and 17 to 40 percent of the gap to their steeper
estimates for 125M and 350M parameters.

\textit{How much of Farseer's convexity could be mis-tuning?} Farseer's runs follow the Step Law rules, so the measured
inefficiency gradients bound what tuning could produce (Table~\ref{tab:app-tuning}). Step Law's own rule changes the
convexity of $\ln w$ by $-4$ to $+8$ percent; untuned configurations, whose inefficiency falls with $D$, would make it larger;
and producing all of it would take an inefficiency that rises with $D$ at 47 times the upper bound of the rule's measured
gradient. The gradients are measured only at $M\le466$; Farseer's grid reaches 2,570.
