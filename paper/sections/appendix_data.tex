\section[Data Construction]{Appendix \thesection. Data Construction}\label{app:data}

This appendix documents the samples in Table~\ref{tab:app-data}: sources, conventions, corrections to source data and
sample restrictions. A single script per module regenerates every sample from the raw files with fixed seeds, and the
replication package lists the download commands. Snapshots of living databases are from September 23 and 24, 2026.

\subsection{Public Sweeps}\label{app:sweeps}

For every sweep we build a run-level panel of $(N,D,L)$ with a cluster identifier, $M=D/N$ and $C=6ND$. We use $C=6ND$
even when a sweep reports a more detailed FLOP count, so that compute means the same thing across sweeps; the model-free
estimator, which needs exact isocosts, is the exception. The sweeps share neither a parameter-count convention, nor a
tokenizer, nor an output. We keep each sweep's conventions and compare only unit-free
objects across sweeps: $\alpha$, $\beta$, $a$, $\gamma$ and $\sigma^*$ are invariant to the unit of loss but not to the
parameter-count convention, while $E$, $A$, $B$ and $M^*$ are not comparable across rows. The off-path spread in
Table~\ref{tab:app-data} is the residual standard deviation of an OLS regression of $\ln M$ on $\ln(6ND)$. Farseer is a
near-factorial grid in $(\ln N,\ln D)$ with $\mathrm{corr}(\ln N,\ln D)=-0.04$; the Chinchilla runs combine nine IsoFLOP
profiles with off-profile streaks at fixed size; Gadre et al.\ and the OLMo ladder are grids in size and token multiplier.

\textit{Farseer.} The released file of \citet{li2025predictableb} contains 404 single-recipe rows: 400 distinct runs of
25 sizes, each on up to 18 token budgets, plus four runs listed twice with identical records (the copies differ only in
a mislabeled \texttt{D/N} column); we keep all 404 rows, and dropping the four copies leaves the statistics of
Table~\ref{tab:app-data} unchanged. Hyperparameters are set by the Step Law rules. $N$ excludes both untied 65,536-row embedding matrices (the reported total
exceeds it by exactly $131{,}072\,h$ for hidden size $h$). The output is English bits per character on the authors'
validation set. The column labeled \texttt{D/N} takes five values unrelated to $D/N$, so we recompute $M$ (0.31--2,570);
the file's compute column includes attention FLOPs, and we use $6ND$ instead.

\textit{Gadre et al., the OLMo ladder and Muennighoff et al.} The 104 runs of \citet{gadre2024language} (34 on C4, 35 on
RedPajama and 35 on RefinedWeb) count all parameters and have $D=20\times\text{multiplier}\times N$; the output is C4
validation loss, on which every model is evaluated, because tests of neutral data quality need a common output. Their
token-level intervals imply a sampling standard error of $\ln L$ of 0.0016, against residual standard deviations of
0.023--0.030 for a fitted Chinchilla form, so residuals reflect misspecification rather than evaluation noise. For the
OLMo ladder we take the final logged step of the 30 runs released with \citet{bhagia2024establishing} in the OLMo-ladder
repository, a superset of the 16-model ladder of their paper: five sizes at 0.5--10 times the Chinchilla budget plus
five reruns, clustered with their originals. $N$ excludes only the input embedding, the output is
C4-en validation cross-entropy, and task bits per byte on the same runs are the second output of Online
Appendix~\ref{app:wedge-output}. From \citet{muennighoff2023scaling} we parse 229 final losses with the authors' code,
rebuild $N$, $D$ and unique tokens from the model names (the aggregated third-party file mislabels two columns), and
keep the 33 single-epoch runs.

\textit{DataDecide.} The release \citep{magnusson2025datadecide} crosses 25 recipes with 14 sizes (4M to 1.2B
parameters without the input embedding) and three seeds (five at 1B), for 1,100 runs in the release; the two auxiliary
seeds of the 530M, 750M and 1B models stop early, at about 89, 49 and 21 tokens per parameter, and we keep them as
truncated runs on the common schedule. We keep 21,888 checkpoints with $D/N\ge5$. Full-length runs end at
$D\approx85$--$110\,N$, and even with checkpoints $\mathrm{corr}(\ln N,\ln D)=0.91$; the checkpoints that
break the collinearity come before the cosine schedule ends, and schedule progress is a function of $D/N$. We therefore
use DataDecide only for comparisons across recipes, which share sizes, steps and schedules, and exclude it by rule from
the technologies of Section~\ref{sec:wedge}. We did not use the release of \citet{kricheli2026tokens}, which contains
only per-epoch training losses.

\subsection{Chinchilla Extraction}\label{app:chinchilla}

The Epoch AI digitization of Figure~4 of \citet{hoffmann2022training} contains 245 runs \citep{besiroglu2024chinchilla},
from $1.4\times10^{18}$ to $1.3\times10^{22}$ FLOP and 57 million to 16 billion parameters. $N$ is the total parameter
count and $C$ is read off the figure's horizontal axis, so $D=C/(6N)$ is constructed: digitization error in $C$ passes
one for one into $\ln D$. We rebuild FLOPs per token for all 50 models of Hoffmann et al.'s Table A9. Their parameter
counts are reproduced, to 0.8 percent, by attention, MLP and one relative-position projection per layer plus one
$32{,}000\times d$ vocabulary matrix. Their Appendix F formula as printed does not reproduce their Table A4; without its
embedding and final-logit terms it reproduces all six ratios. In that count, FLOPs per token are 0.98--1.15 times $6N$
for the models in the profiles. The within-budget pattern of the digitized FLOP coordinates is flat once the budgets are
read in that count (slope $-0.002$, s.e.\ 0.007) and not in the printed count ($-0.065$), in executed FLOPs ($-0.036$) or
in $6ND$ ($+0.020$). This is what one expects if Figure~4 plots runs at $6ND$ with token counts set in Hoffmann et al.'s
count. Then the digitization's $D=C/(6N)$ is the true token count, each profile is an exact isocost in that count, and
$N_F=C/(6D)$ is FLOPs per token over six, whatever $D$ (Online Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app-chinflop}). The 44M model
of Table A9 does not appear in the digitization. The digitized offset of $\log_{10}C$ below the nominal budgets
($-0.018$; below) is mostly this accounting: net of it, $-0.006$ remains. Drawn on the corrected coordinates, the
membership rule below gives 147 profile runs instead of 137, and $\sigma^*=0.668$ (Online
Appendix~\ref{app:add-modelfree}). The loss is Hoffmann et al.'s smoothed final training loss on MassiveText, in nats
per token (each token is seen once, so it is an unbiased estimate of held-out loss), read from a color map and quantized
to about $\pm0.01$. Besiroglu et al.\ drop the five highest-loss runs, all from the $10^{19}$ budget with 2.0 to 6.8 billion
parameters, and so do we ($n=240$).

Digitized $\log_{10}C$ sits systematically 0.018 below the nine nominal budgets, $6\times10^{18}$ to $3\times10^{21}$
FLOP; we estimate this offset as the median deviation of runs within 0.1 of a budget and remove it. A run belongs to an
IsoFLOP profile if its corrected $\log_{10}C$ is within 0.045 of a budget, which gives 137 profile runs of 245 (132 of
240). The other 108 runs form 35 streaks of fixed model size, the training-horizon runs that Hoffmann et al.\ pool into
their Approach~3. In the 240-run sample, 33 percent of the variance of centered $(\ln N,\ln D)$ lies across the expansion
path. Assigning every run to its nearest budget, as a nine-cluster bootstrap requires, puts off-profile runs from a
$10^{22}$-FLOP trunk into the $3\times10^{21}$ IsoFLOP experiment; our cluster schemes therefore group profile runs by
budget and off-profile runs by their model-size trunk. Both Chinchilla series in the compilation of
\citet{openathena2026isoflop}, associated with \citet{czech2026problems} (123 and 124 points), are snapped subsets of the
same digitization, not independent sources.

\subsection{Laboratory IsoFLOP Profiles and Hyperparameter Sweeps}\label{app:isoflop}

\textit{Llama~3.} The 133 points of Meta's IsoFLOP figure \citep{grattafiori2024llama} come from the digitization of
\citet{czech2026llama3isoflop}, distributed in the compilation of \citet{openathena2026isoflop}, associated with
\citet{czech2026problems}; the two agree row by row.
There are ten budgets from $6\times10^{18}$ to $10^{22}$ FLOP. Two of them, $3\times10^{21}$ and $10^{22}$, have six
runs each and do not bracket their minimum with two runs on each side; the model-free estimator and the anchors of Online
Appendix~\ref{app:wedge-pi} use the other eight, and Meta's ten-budget planning law is a sensitivity. $D$ is read off
the figure and $N=C/(6D)$ uses the nominal budget. The loss units are not stated, but the model-free estimator and the
wedge are invariant to monotone transformations of the output.

\textit{Marin.} The scaling ladders of \citet{marin2026ladders}, in the March-2026 W\&B export distributed in the
compilation of \citet{openathena2026isoflop} (experiments marin\_202603), train a Llama-2-style architecture on three corpora
(Comma, DCLM and Nemotron-CC): 85, 85 and 88 runs on seven or eight budgets from $1.8\times10^{18}$ to $3\times10^{20}$
FLOP, with the macro-averaged Paloma loss as output. The nominal budgets are three times the forward FLOPs and differ from
$6ND$ with the configuration parameter count by $-7$ to $+35$ percent, a difference strongly negatively correlated with
$N$ within a budget. Model-free estimates use $N=C_b/(6D)$, which makes each profile an exact isocost; with the
configuration count they are 0.04 to 0.07 lower. The checkpoint-interpolated profiles that \citet{czech2026problems}
construct from the training logs of \citet{li2025misfitting}, whose points come before the learning-rate schedule ends,
enter only the duality tests.

\textit{Porian et al.\ and Step Law.} The released results of \citet{porian2024resolving} contain 975 runs of 16
architectures on RefinedWeb and OpenWebText2. We count parameters and FLOPs without the output head (Kaplan), with it
(standard), with attention FLOPs and with all embeddings, and our re-implementation of their IsoFLOP pipeline reproduces
their path estimates to within 0.003. The 264 runs that reproduce Kaplan et al.'s setup use cosine decay, either to zero
at a fixed horizon of about 131B tokens or to 1 percent at each target budget; the other 711, the 61 with tuned
hyperparameters (among them the 32 of their final configuration, whose IsoFLOP profiles we use), their hyperparameter
sweeps and their seed replicates, use a constant learning rate without cooldown. The cosine runs enter only the decomposition of Online
Appendix~\ref{app:add-measurement}. The dense-model file of \citet{li2025predictablea}
contains 1,911 runs in 17 $(N,D)$ cells ($N$ 215M--1.07B non-embedding parameters, $D$ 4B--100B, $M$ 19--466), each with a
grid of up to 12 learning rates and 10 batch sizes; the output is the smoothed final training loss, and we classify 9.5
percent of runs as diverged (log loss more than 0.30 above the cell minimum).

\subsection{The Controlled Experiment}\label{app:sweep}

\textit{Corpora and tokenizer.} The corpora are FineWeb-Edu and FineWeb \citep{penedo2024fineweb}; FineWeb-Edu keeps the
documents of the same crawl that an educational-quality classifier rates highly. The first 20,000 documents of each
sample-10BT release are held out for validation, and the training tokens exceed every budget, so no run repeats data. A
BPE tokenizer \citep{sennrich2016neural} over raw bytes with 8,192 tokens, trained on both corpora and shared, puts
losses in the same units; we report bits per byte (3.89 and 3.71 bytes per token in the scored tokens of the FineWeb-Edu
and FineWeb validation sets).

\textit{Architecture and training.} The models are decoder-only transformers with RMSNorm pre-normalization
\citep{zhang2019root}, rotary position embeddings \citep{su2024roformer}, a four-times-wide GELU feed-forward block and an
input embedding tied to the output projection; width $d_{\rm model}$ sets $d_{\rm model}/64$ layers and heads
(Table~\ref{tab:app_sweepdesign}). The tied embedding is 73 percent of all parameters at width 128 and 10 percent at 640,
so the counting convention matters far more than in production models \citep{pearce2024reconciling,porian2024resolving}.
All runs but one (described below) use AdamW \citep{loshchilov2019decoupled} with weight decay 0.1, which the
implementation (MLX) multiplies by the learning rate, so its averaging timescale relative to run length varies across the design. The learning rate warms up over
250 steps and stays constant on a trunk; for each budget $D_k$ the state is copied at $0.8D_k$ and the copy is cooled to
zero \citep{hagele2024scaling}, so one trunk delivers a column of the design and every endpoint is annealed. Endpoints of
one shape share the trunk, so inference clusters by width. The peak learning rate follows
$\mathrm{lr}^{*}(d_{\rm model})=3.07\times10^{-3}(d_{\rm model}/256)^{-0.90}$, calibrated at widths 128, 320 and 512 on 50
million FineWeb-Edu tokens. All models were trained in MLX \citep{hannun2023mlx} on one Apple M5 Max.

\textit{Additions.} Four further sets of runs measure noise and tuning: the learning-rate calibration repeated on FineWeb;
peak rates of 0.5 and 2 times the rule at width 128 over the full budget ladder and at width 640 at 25 and 50 million
tokens, on both corpora; seed replicates (widths 128, 256 and 384 with two further seeds at 50--200 million tokens, and,
at width 128, the first replicate seed continued to 200--800 million tokens), each changing initialization and data
order; and, at the four-layer width-128 high-$M$ shape on FineWeb-Edu, rates of 0.5 and 2 times the rule and one run
whose weight decay is scaled to hold the timescale-to-run-length ratio at its main-grid median. The high-$M$ tuning
checks exist for FineWeb-Edu only and stop at 1.6 billion tokens. Every endpoint except the 12 FineWeb-Edu
learning-rate calibration runs, which were evaluated on FineWeb-Edu only, is evaluated on the first 1,048,576 tokens of
both validation sets, and the added runs other than the FineWeb-Edu seed replicates also on WikiText-103
\citep{merity2016pointer} during training.

\textit{Neutral sets and trunk evaluations.} Runs launched after the trainer update of 17:44 on 24 September 2026 log the
unannealed trunk's validation loss at every branch point except the last and save their annealed endpoint weights. The
evaluation sets are fixed when a queue's job list is generated. The lists for the FineWeb main grid (15:37) and the
FineWeb-Edu high-$M$ runs (17:33) predate the update, so during training the FineWeb main grid was evaluated only on the
two in-distribution sets and those high-$M$ runs also on WikiText-103; the exception is FineWeb's width-640 endpoint at
200 million tokens, whose trunk was rerun after a machine reboot from a list generated after the update. Runs from later
job lists, other than the FineWeb-Edu seed replicates, are evaluated on C4 and PG-19 as well. Where an endpoint's weights
were saved but a neutral set is missing (FineWeb main-grid widths 320 to 640, the FineWeb-Edu $(256,4)$ high-$M$ shape and
the FineWeb-Edu seed replicates), the missing losses are computed after training from those weights with the same
evaluation code (deviation D13); each such evaluation recomputes the recorded losses as a check on the reload, and the
first check reproduced them exactly. The FineWeb-Edu main grid and the FineWeb main grid at widths 128 to 256 have no
saved weights and no neutral-set losses, and the FineWeb-Edu $(128,4)$ high-$M$ shape has WikiText-103 losses but no C4 or
PG-19 losses. A between-corpus tilt on the neutral sets is therefore not identified on the main grid: the main-grid
verdict on neutrality rests on the two in-distribution validation sets, and the neutral sets enter through matched-cell
sign checks. C4 is lightly filtered web text, closer to FineWeb than to FineWeb-Edu, so it is not neutral between the
corpora \textcolor{red}{[TBD-m9: endpoints with post hoc neutral-set losses]}. To supply matched cells at the rule's
learning rate beyond width 128, FineWeb-Edu's main-grid trunks at widths 384 and 512 are retrained, with the same seed and
data order, with every evaluation set, trunk evaluations and saved weights (Amendment~2); the original endpoints stay in
the fits. Unannealed trunk losses thus exist for FineWeb widths 320 to 640 (up to 400 million tokens, so $M\le65$ in
convention P), for FineWeb-Edu widths 384 and 512 from the retrained trunks, for the seed replicates, and for the
high-$M$ shapes other than FineWeb-Edu's $(128,4)$ run at the rule, never at a trunk's last budget.

\textit{Pre-analysis plan, deviations and power.} The pre-analysis plan, which is in the replication package, was
committed at 03:12 (UTC+3) on 24 September 2026 (commit \texttt{bd5c0ad}); its header gives 03:15 as the time of
writing, three minutes after the commit. At that moment part of the FineWeb-Edu main grid had finished and its raw
endpoint losses had appeared in run logs, but no model had been fitted; the plan's primary choices, including the
non-embedding convention for the extrapolation question, were therefore made after those logs existed. The FineWeb main
grid and the high-$M$ runs had not started, and no addition had produced a result: the first addition, the FineWeb
learning-rate calibration, was launched 46 seconds before the commit, and its first endpoint finished more than two
minutes after it. The plan fixes the output, both parameter conventions, the robustness samples, the estimators, the
inference (a wild cluster bootstrap by width and a parametric bootstrap with the within-trunk covariance from the seed
replicates), six questions and a commitment to report every answer. Only the neutrality question (Q2) has a decision
rule; Q1 is a test of equal curvature across corpora, Q3 a signed statistic, and Q4--Q6 are descriptive.

The power, size and coverage of the plan's inference were computed from the design alone between 15:52 and 17:03, before
the first estimation at 17:12 (Table~\ref{tab:app-power1}). The plan's wild cluster bootstrap with eight width clusters
is anti-conservative on this design: its intervals cover 40--83 percent at a nominal 95, and its test of a zero tilt
rejects a true null about 20 percent of the time. That study used 40 to 150 replications. A second study from the design
alone, run on 25 September for Amendment~2 before any FineWeb technology estimate, uses 500 replications for the tilt test
and for coverage at a noise s.d.\ of 0.005 (150 to 200 in sensitivity cells), adds the FLOP-effective convention and
$\kappa$-family truths, and gives a Monte Carlo standard error for every rate (Table~\ref{tab:app-power}). It shows that
the restricted test of D8 is less reliable than 40 replications suggested: on one validation set it rejects a true zero
tilt 9.2 percent of the time (Monte Carlo s.e.\ 1.3 points) and a tilt of 0.22 only 65 percent of the time, because the
residuals of the fit that imposes a zero tilt carry the tilt; its two-set rule calls a tilt of 0.22 neutral 15 percent
of the time. Amendment~2 therefore uses, in the CR2 scheme, the CR2 interval with its half-width multiplied by 1.25. Its
two-set rule calls a tilt of 0.22 factor-biased in every replication, and a true zero tilt factor-biased 5.4 percent of
the time, against 3.0 percent for D8's rule. At that noise level the model-free $\sigma^*$ in FLOP-effective parameters has a
Monte Carlo standard deviation of 0.019 on FineWeb-Edu's design and 0.014 on FineWeb's. The CR2 scheme's intervals for
the model-free $\sigma^*$ in FLOP-effective parameters cover 74 percent at a noise
s.d.\ of 0.005, and 92 percent once widened by 1.74, the largest calibration factor across noise levels, within-trunk
correlations, conventions and truths (85 percent at a within-trunk share of 0.9); the factor is 1.75 for the Q3 slope and 1.64 for the difference in $\sigma^*$
between corpora, whose smallest value detectable with 80 percent power is then 0.075. The null value of the Q3 slope is
not zero: at noise s.d.\ 0.002 to 0.010 it is $-0.01$ to $-0.09$ in convention P, and in convention T it is $+0.11$ with
the high-$M$ runs, or $-0.08$ on the main grid without noise ($-0.08$ to $-0.12$ at noise s.d.\ 0.002--0.005), because
the Chinchilla form is misspecified in total-parameter units when it holds in non-embedding units. Q3 is therefore read
against a design-specific null, recomputed at the noise measured in Q4 (Amendment~2).

\subsection{Released Models}\label{app:choices}

\textit{The Epoch universe.} From the 2,070 language-model rows of the Epoch AI database \citep{epochai2026data}
(snapshot of September 23, 2026) we exclude 1,728, among them fine-tunes and other derived models, rows without $N$ or a
recoverable token count, multimodal and non-text models, encoders, benchmark-scale research models and 29 manual exclusions
with recorded reasons; the replication package lists every count. $D$ is dataset size times epochs; seven word counts are
converted to tokens, 67 rows with operation-counted compute but no dataset size get $D=C/(6N)$, and six token counts are
corrected from primary sources (GPT-3 175B, 300B tokens; GLM-130B, 400B).

\textit{The verified sample.} It starts from the 148 base models of the ObsScaling evaluation file
\citep{ruan2024observational}, with nine documented corrections (for example, OPT's $D$ is 300B tokens, not its 180B-token
corpus), drops 17 models with undisclosed or ambiguous token counts or that are upcycled, duplicated or
instruction-tuned, replaces the deduplicated Pythia suite with the standard one \citep{biderman2023pythia}, and adds 57
models released between September 2023 and November 2025 whose token counts come from model cards or technical
reports. Excluding 15
code-specialized models leaves 173 general-purpose models. For 143 of the 188 models (131 of the 173 general-purpose ones) $N$ is the exact count of floating-point
tensors in the Hugging Face checkpoint, excluding integer buffers such as stored causal masks; mixture-of-experts models
enter with active parameters, and configuration files give the non-embedding $N$. For OLMo~2 we count pretraining
tokens plus the mean mid-training budget of the runs averaged into each released model (the report's totals, which add
every averaged run, are 5.6 and 6.6 trillion tokens for the 13B and 32B). MPT-30B is 1.05 trillion tokens (1 trillion at
2k context and 50 billion at 8k). Yi uses 3 trillion tokens, as in its report's over-training statement and model card;
the report also gives 3.1 trillion.

\textit{The clean sample and decision units.} Nine steps, listed with their sources in Online
Appendix~\ref{app:wedge-sample}, reduce the verified sample to 77 models from 36 families and 18 developers, released
2023--2025. An audit of the clean sample's token counts against primary sources corrected StableLM-Alpha: 800 billion
training tokens, not the 1.5-trillion-token dataset size, and 3.64 and 7.87 billion parameters, not the nominal 3 and 7
billion; no headline median moves. Qwen3's report documents the tokens of every size, but Qwen2.5 states only
``up to 18 trillion'' tokens for all sizes, so its members' $D$ is flagged as unaudited and robustness rows drop both
families. A decision unit is one choice of $(N,D)$: the 11 families that train every size on token budgets within 10
percent of each other (32 models) are one unit each, and the 30 members of families with size-specific budgets and the 15
singletons are one unit each, for 56 units. For each common-budget family the technical reports and release posts are
read for whether the budget was chosen, set by a binding corpus cap, or accompanied by sizes from a hardware menu (Online
Appendix~\ref{app:wedge-family}); every quote is saved with its URL and retrieval time and checked against the saved
source.

\textit{Serving at the model level.} A model is coded as served if a first-party per-token API or consumer product
offered this size (any post-trained variant of the same run) within 180 days of its release. The evidence is dated
first-party pages, Internet Archive captures of Alibaba's model catalogs, and dated press. Self-deploy catalogs (Vertex AI
Model Garden, the Azure AI Studio catalog), where the customer rents the accelerators, and research demos (Hugging Face
Spaces, the Ai2 Playground) are coded 0 and kept as flags. Twenty-six of the 77 models are served, 19 of them Alibaba's.
Of the 77 codes, 48 rest on the model's own dated evidence, 13 on a sibling's from the same release, 2 on an undated page
of the same developer, and 14, all coded 0, on the developer-level coding of the previous version without a model-level
source; every code of 1 has a dated, quote-verified source. Three sizes are ambiguous (Llama~3 8B and 70B behind ``Meta
AI, built with Llama~3 technology'', and Yi-34B's unnamed early-access API); they are coded 0, and 1 in a sensitivity. A model
has an on-device target if its card names on-device, mobile or phone use.

\textit{Production-scale universe.} Trends and the open-weight comparison use models trained with $6ND\ge10^{21}$ FLOP:
Epoch rows that are not speculative, not code models and not matched to the verified sample (176), plus the verified
general-purpose models above the threshold (159), for 335 models from 94 developers after name variants are merged. The
open-closed comparison uses the 186 with confident parameter and token counts, without mixture-of-experts or
non-transformer architectures and with the clean-sample exclusions applied to verified models; the bunching test uses
the 220 open-weight models released in 2023--2026 with their total parameter counts.

\textit{Derivatives and units.} From the Hugging Face model tree we count, for 74 clean models, the quantizations,
fine-tunes, adapters and merges derived from the base repository and official releases. Wedges are computed in each
technology's own parameter convention, with $D$ in each model's own tokens; under the reference technology
$\partial\ln w/\partial\ln D=0.43$, so a 20 percent difference in tokenizer efficiency moves $\ln w$ by about 0.08.
