\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:concl}

Optimization cuts both ways for measurement. Developers who minimize cost generate data that say little about the
technology they optimize over (Proposition~\ref{prop:ident}) and choices that say a good deal about what they optimize
for (Proposition~\ref{prop:wedge}). Designed experiments break the circle. Wherever the cost of two inputs is known
and experiments vary them along isocosts (with a correction when the cost is not multiplicative), as in agronomic
factorial trials, three results carry over: a quantified version of the argument of \citet{marschak1944random} and
\citet{bond2005adjustment}; an estimator of the elasticity of substitution that needs no functional form; and a
revealed-preference result, of use for the production approach to markups \citep{deloecker2012markups}, that signs a
first-order-condition wedge from the location of the optimum alone, where experiments locate it
(Proposition~\ref{prop:modelfree}).

Applied to language models, they deliver three findings. The elasticity of substitution between parameters and data is
about 0.7 at $10^{19}$ to $3\times10^{20}$ FLOP, where the designs overlap, and about 0.6 at the largest budgets of
the two IsoFLOP designs that go beyond. Most dense open-weight models released since 2023 are trained beyond
training-cost minimization, though not most training compute. And the over-training their developers reveal rose
steeply from 2023 to 2025, weighted by compute under every technology we consider; its level depends on the
technology. \textcolor{red}{[TBD-m9: one sentence on what our experiment adds: $\sigma^*$ by corpus and the
FineWeb-Edu versus FineWeb neutrality verdict.]} The object on which the designs agree most closely, $\sigma^*$, does
not enter compute-optimal data demand and barely moves the cost of a data wall unless repeated data decay fast; it
matters by scaling the revealed value of compactness. The least portable objects, the allocation exponent and the
compute-optimal ratio at frontier scale, matter most; laboratories could resolve them by publishing bracketed IsoFLOP
minima at frontier compute.

\textit{What economists should use.} For substitution between FLOP-effective parameters and tokens, 0.68 at $10^{20}$
FLOP from the pooled meta-regression (95 percent interval 0.64 to 0.72), and 0.59 at $6\times10^{20}$ to
$3\times10^{21}$ FLOP in Chinchilla and Llama~3. As a summary over the designs' budgets, one estimate per study gives
0.69 (0.64 to 0.73). Beyond the designs $\sigma^*$ is partially identified: if it neither rises with compute nor falls
faster than inside the designs, it lies between 0.42 and 0.70 at $10^{24}$ FLOP (at most 0.59 if the top-budget
decline is real). For growth calibrations at $10^{25}$ to $10^{27}$ FLOP, $\gamma=0.165$, or 66 times the compute per
halving of reducible loss. For data demand, the range across technologies, not one path. For the value of compactness,
its sign and its rise rather than its level: zero-point allowances lower the level, a curvature that keeps falling
with compute raises it at fixed zero points, and binding caps on tokens make it a lower bound. The sign is identified
beyond the designs for 78 percent of clean-sample models, given the designs' paths extrapolated with their sampling
error, but for only 24 percent of their training compute.

\textit{Implications for scaling studies.} Studies that aim to measure the technology should place runs off the
expansion path, bracket the loss minimum at every budget and test rather than impose $\kappa=1$. They should anneal
learning-rate schedules, tune hyperparameters by size and token budget
\citep{tissue2024scaling,bergsma2025power,lourie2026small}, and state their FLOP and parameter conventions.

\textit{Limitations.} First, units: only unit-free objects compare across designs. Second, extrapolation: 76 of the 77
clean-sample models were trained above $10^{21}$ FLOP. Parametric forms understate the wedge at high tokens per
parameter inside three recipes' designs, but the direction beyond them is recipe-dependent and nothing is observed at
frontier compute. \textcolor{red}{[TBD-m9: whether our high-$M$ runs confirm this direction.]} A curvature that falls
with compute also breaks the quasi-homotheticity the inversion assumes. Third, developers that do not disclose
training tokens, most closed ones among them, are absent. Fourth, the wedge leaves out post-training compute, which
moves the median share by about 0.02 at the largest disclosed amount, and the gap between loss and capability:
over-trained models degrade more under quantization \citep{kumar2024scaling}. Fifth, the data-wall results rest on
repetition estimates from models of at most nine billion parameters.

Designed training runs let production-function econometrics check what optimizing agents reveal, and what they
conceal, against experimental variation.
