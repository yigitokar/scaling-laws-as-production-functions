\begin{tablenotes}
The eleven families of the clean inference-demand sample whose members were trained on token budgets within 10 percent
of each other (32 models). Wedges under the reference technology. With a common $D$ and sizes chosen at the margin, the
family's first-order condition in $D$ identifies one number, a weighted average of the members' values of compactness:
$\sum_i\pi_im_{N,i}=\bar w_H-1$ without data costs, where $\pi_i\propto\omega_i/w_i$, $\omega_i$ is member $i$'s share
of family training compute and $\bar w_H=[\sum_i\omega_i/w_i]^{-1}$ is the compute-weighted harmonic mean of the member
wedges, computed from the technology (Proposition~\ref{prop:wedge}(iii); Online Appendix
Proposition~\ref{prop:A-family}); $1-1/\bar w_H=\sum_i\omega_is_i$ is the family share. Member wedges then differ only through the technology, $\ln w_i-\ln w_j=-\alpha(\ln N_i-\ln N_j)$, and are not
revealed-preference objects. If sizes come from a menu, the condition in $D$ says nothing about the members' values of
compactness. In three family problems solved by brute force, the condition holds to within $2.2\times10^{-8}$.
\end{tablenotes}
