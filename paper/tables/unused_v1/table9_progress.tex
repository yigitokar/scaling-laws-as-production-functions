Rows (1)--(2) minimize their objective, mean squared error plus $0.0025\sum|\theta|$, by SLSQP from zero; row (1) uses the scipy default tolerance of the authors' code and row (2) runs to convergence. The published 8.4 months is a bootstrap median; the point estimate of row (1) is 8.7. Intervals: 95\% paper-cluster bootstrap (400 draws), percentiles of $g_N+g_D$ mapped to $T_C$ ($\infty$ when non-positive growth lies inside the interval); the published interval is the authors' iid bootstrap; the profile-likelihood interval inverts an iid Gaussian likelihood-ratio test. Hicks neutrality: $\alpha g_N=\beta g_D$. (7): data measured as effective data under repetition \citep{muennighoff2023scaling}. (8): the frontier elasticity obtained by fitting the $E=0$ form to the Chinchilla sweep. (9): Ho et al.'s year coefficients divided by the exponents of \citet{besiroglu2024chinchilla}, without re-estimation \citep{whitfill2025note}. (10)--(12): exponents fixed at experimental values, benchmark-specific $E_b$ estimated; row (12) reports the range over the 12 technologies estimated in Section~\ref{sec:tech} (two on the Chinchilla sweep): $g_C<0$ for 9 of them; for the other 3, $T_C$ lies between 42 and 122 months, and every interval includes non-positive growth. Panel B: $\overline{CE}$ is the geometric mean of Farrell cost efficiency $C_{\min}(L(N,D))/6ND$ under the stated technology in each period ($n_1$, $n_2$ models); gain $=\overline{CE}_2/\overline{CE}_1$, with a bootstrap over models (and over parameter draws for Besiroglu et al.). Share $=\ln(\text{gain})/\ln(8.9)$, where 8.9 is the effective-compute gain implied by row (1) over the difference in mean release dates (9.9 for the top-five sample, 8.2 for the cleaned sample); a negative share means that cost efficiency fell. Top five: the five models with the most compute in each year. Cleaned: drops derivatives, systems-paper runs, non-transformers, exact re-releases, models with $D/N$ exactly 20 and Epoch ``speculative'' entries.
