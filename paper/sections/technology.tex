\section{The Technology from Designed Variation}\label{sec:tech}

This section takes Proposition~\ref{prop:modelfree} to the data. Throughout, $\sigma^*$ is the elasticity of
substitution on the expansion path between FLOP-effective parameters, $N_F=C/(6D)$, and training tokens. On every
public IsoFLOP design with annealed runs and on Farseer's factorial grid, $\sigma^*$ is about 0.70 where the designs
overlap, $10^{19}$ to $3\times10^{20}$ FLOP. It falls to about 0.59 at the largest budgets of the two IsoFLOP designs
that go beyond (Farseer's local path gives 0.66 at $10^{21}$ FLOP), and past the designs it is only partially
identified. Where the same-run comparison discriminates, on Llama~3 and Farseer, only the $\kappa$-free form agrees with
the model-free benchmark. Neither $a$ nor $M^*$ is portable across designs, conventions and data.

\subsection{Designs}\label{sec:tech:designs}\label{sec:tech:chinchilla}

Table~\ref{tab:designs} lists the designs. Four studies provide IsoFLOP profiles, models of different sizes trained on
the isocost line $6ND=C$ at each of several budgets. Epoch AI's digitization of the runs of \citet{hoffmann2022training},
the sample of \citet{besiroglu2024chinchilla}, has 137 runs on nine IsoFLOP profiles from $6\times10^{18}$ to
$3\times10^{21}$ FLOP and 108 from 35 fixed-size trunks; parametric fits drop the five with the highest loss ($n=240$).
Meta's Llama~3 profiles \citep{grattafiori2024llama}, digitized by \citet{czech2026llama3isoflop}, cover ten budgets up
to $10^{22}$ FLOP, of which the eight up to $10^{21}$ bracket their minimum; their unstated loss units do not matter,
because the estimator is invariant to monotone transformations of output. Marin's ladders \citep{marin2026ladders}
repeat the design on three corpora up to $3\times10^{20}$ FLOP. \citet{porian2024resolving} release loss curves of
architectures trained at a constant learning rate, from which we read profiles at 12 budgets up to $2.6\times10^{19}$
FLOP; these runs are not annealed.

The 404 released runs of Farseer \citep{li2025predictableb} form a near-factorial grid dense enough to locate the
expansion path. Three smaller sweeps have no profiles: the three web corpora of \citet{gadre2024language}, the OLMo
ladder \citep{bhagia2024establishing} and the single-epoch runs of \citet{muennighoff2023scaling}; with Chinchilla's full
design and Farseer, they form seven sweep--corpus technologies. DataDecide \citep{magnusson2025datadecide} serves only to
compare data recipes. The model-free estimator needs exact isocosts, so $N$ is counted so that $C=6ND$ holds along each
profile: $N_F=C/(6D)$, with $C$ each study's own FLOP count, for Chinchilla, Llama~3 and Marin, and the non-embedding
count for Farseer. For Chinchilla we rebuild FLOPs per token from Hoffmann et al.'s architecture table (Online Appendix
Table~\ref{tab:app-chinflop}). The off-path spread, the counterpart of $\varsigma$ in Proposition~\ref{prop:ident}, is
1.4--2.5 in the IsoFLOP designs and 0.94 on the OLMo ladder.

\subsection{The Elasticity without a Functional Form}\label{sec:tech:modelfree}

To apply equation~\eqref{eq:ident-mf}, a quadratic in $\ln N$ fitted to the runs within one log point of the path gives
the curvature at each budget, a cubic in $\ln C$ through the logged minima gives the slope of the IsoFLOP-minima
frontier, and a design's estimate is the random-effects mean over budgets \citep{dersimonian1986meta}, with standard
errors from a design-conditional wild bootstrap that re-estimates every step. On Farseer we fit a kernel-weighted local
quadratic of $\ln L$ in $(\ln N,\ln D)$, locate the points where the local wedge equals one, and read $\sigma^*$ off the
slope of $\ln w$ in $\ln M$ there, which equals $1/\sigma^*-1$ for any smooth technology
(Proposition~\ref{prop:modelfree}). Online Appendix~\ref{app:additional} gives details and robustness.

\textit{Levels.} In FLOP-effective parameters, $\sigma^*$ is 0.660 (standard error 0.023) on Chinchilla's profiles,
0.660 (0.023) on Meta's and 0.700, 0.713 and 0.705 on Marin's three corpora (Figure~\ref{fig:sigma}, panel~a); Farseer's
local expansion path gives 0.708. Marin's corpora share code, ladder and FLOP accounting, so we treat them as one study.
With one estimate per study, the random-effects mean is 0.687, with a 95 percent confidence interval of [0.640, 0.734]
(Table~\ref{tab:designs}, panel~C). Chinchilla and Meta give 0.66 and Marin and Farseer 0.71, but the evidence that
they differ is borderline: Cochran's statistic is 8.3 on three degrees of freedom ($p=0.04$) and ranges from 4.4 to
14.8 across Chinchilla's windows, samples and profile memberships.

The interval reflects sampling error only. FLOP accounting, parameter conventions, Chinchilla's windows and samples, and
estimators move the mean between 0.663 and 0.702, and several of these variants have intervals that reach about 0.60
(Table~\ref{tab:designs}, panel~C). Without noise, the estimator's bias on each design's fitted technologies is
at most 0.006; with noise, at the fitted $\kappa$-family technologies of three designs, its interval covers the truth 93
to 95 percent of the time, and 93 to 94 percent when half the noise variance is shared within a budget (Online
Appendix~\ref{app:add-modelfree}).

Porian et al.'s profiles are the exception, at 0.518 (0.017) and 0.505 (0.014), and we keep them out of the summary.
If the loss gain from annealing, which depends on the whole learning-rate path
\citep{hagele2024scaling,tissue2024scaling}, varies with $D/N$ at fixed compute, it bends the profile. Their estimates move between 0.45 and 0.55 across windows of 0.8 to 1.5 log points
(0.33--0.34 with a global quadratic), and unlike the annealed designs they rise with compute ($+0.040$ per decade,
standard error 0.006). The exclusion rests on a hypothesis about schedules, which our experiment tests.

\textit{Scale.} Over the 44 budget-level estimates of Figure~\ref{fig:sigma}, panel~b, a three-level random-effects meta-regression
\citep{konstantopoulos2011fixed} gives a slope of $-0.033$ per decade of compute, but the evidence of a common drift is
weak: with four design clusters (Marin as one study) or six, no cluster-robust $p$-value is reliable
\citep{bell2002bias,pustejovsky2018small}, and across weighting, scale, test and design fixed effects the $p$-value lies
between 0.03 and 0.49. The pooled line gives $\sigma^*=0.713$ at $10^{19}$ FLOP, 0.680 at $10^{20}$ and 0.647 at
$10^{21}$.

At or below $3\times10^{20}$ FLOP, where every design has budgets, the pooled
slope is $-0.008$, although the designs' own slopes differ: Llama~3 already falls by 0.050 per decade and gives 0.618
(0.013) at $3\times10^{20}$ FLOP, the budget where Marin stops, not a break the data locate. Above it only Chinchilla and
Llama~3 have bracketed budgets. Across their 17 budgets $\sigma^*$ falls by 0.058 per decade (standard error
0.014, from joint bootstrap draws scaled for excess dispersion), and their five budgets at $6\times10^{20}$ FLOP and above
agree ($Q=2.5$, $p=0.65$) and give $\sigma^*=0.594$, with a 95 percent confidence interval of [0.561, 0.627], 85 percent
of the weight from Llama~3. Farseer's local path gives 0.658 (0.011) at $10^{21}$ FLOP; with it the six top estimates
are heterogeneous ($Q=21.1$, $p<0.001$). The pooled drift loses significance without either design or without each
design's largest budget, and both designs are digitized from figures. Marin shows no drift, but a design of its size
detects a drift of $-0.06$ per decade only 31 percent of the time. The drift does not look like an artifact of the
estimator: in simulations on three designs' grids it creates none and attenuates an imposed one by at most 20 percent,
and symmetric windows or a power-law frontier leave the Chinchilla--Llama~3 drift between $-0.049$ and $-0.064$ per
decade (Online Appendix~\ref{app:add-modelfree}).

\textit{Beyond the designs.} All but one of the 77 models in the clean sample of Section~\ref{sec:wedge} were trained
with more than $10^{21}$ FLOP, and 73 with more than $3\times10^{21}$, Chinchilla's largest bracketed budget; there
$\sigma^*$ is at best partially identified \citep{manski2003partial}. If $\sigma^*$ does not rise with compute and falls
no faster than the drift within the designs, $\sigma^*(C)$ lies between a linear continuation of the Chinchilla--Llama~3
drift and 0.70, the level where the designs overlap (0.59, the top-budget value, if the decline there is real;
Figure~\ref{fig:sigma}, panel~c). The lower end is 0.48 at $10^{23}$ FLOP and 0.42 at $10^{24}$ with the
top-budget value placed at $10^{21}$ FLOP (0.49 and 0.43 at the budgets' compute-weighted center). Neither assumption
follows from the data: a hinge at $3\times10^{20}$ FLOP gives a decline of 0.15 per decade above the knot, and nothing
rules out a reversal.

The curvature prices departures from the compute-optimal mix. In the $\kappa$ family with equal exponents, a
model trained on ten times the compute-optimal tokens per parameter needs 73 percent more compute than the
compute-optimal model of the same loss at $\sigma^*=0.70$, and 124 percent more at 0.60 (72--75 and 122--132 percent
with the published splits of the exponents; Online Appendix~\ref{app:add-overhead}).

\subsection{Parametric Forms}\label{sec:tech:parametric}\label{sec:tech:sweeps}

Scaling laws are usually fitted in the Chinchilla form, the $\kappa=1$ member of family \eqref{eq:tech}, which ties the
curvature of IsoFLOP profiles to the slopes of the frontier and the path (Proposition~\ref{prop:modelfree}). The
restriction is rejected wherever it can be tested. On Chinchilla's full design $\hat\kappa=0.774$, and $\kappa=1$ is
rejected at 1 percent under five of six inference schemes; it is
rejected in all seven sweep--corpus technologies and on every IsoFLOP design, on Chinchilla's profile runs only in
FLOP-effective parameters (quasi-likelihood ratio 12.4, against 7.8 in total parameters and a critical value of 8.6 on
the full design). Where the same-run comparison discriminates, only the $\kappa$-free form agrees with the model-free
benchmark (Figure~\ref{fig:sigma}, panel~a): on Llama~3, $\kappa=1$ gives 0.769, 4.8 standard errors above the
model-free 0.660, and $\kappa$ free 0.693; on Farseer, $\kappa=1$ gives 0.772, against 0.708 on the local path and
0.710 with $\kappa$ free. On Chinchilla's full design (240 runs, total parameters) the two
forms give 0.737 and 0.701, and the bootstrap-calibrated profile-likelihood set with $\kappa$ free, [0.68, 0.72],
excludes 0.74; on Marin the Chinchilla form is slightly the closer.

With $\kappa$ free, the three sweeps without profiles give lower and heterogeneous values (Table~\ref{tab:designs},
panel~B). With 30--35 runs, $E$ and $\kappa$ are hard to separate (Online Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app-kappa-robust}), but
the OLMo ladder is precise and still low, so a difference in recipe cannot be ruled out. Section~\ref{sec:wedge}
therefore uses as its reference the $\kappa$-free Chinchilla technology, whose $\sigma^*$ of 0.701 (0.700 refitted in
$N_F$) lies inside the study-level interval.

\subsection{What Is Not Portable}\label{sec:tech:portable}\label{sec:tech:quality}\label{sec:tech:measurement}

The inversion of Section~\ref{sec:wedge} also needs a zero point, $M^*(C)$, and this is where designs disagree. Under
the Chinchilla form, the allocation exponent $a$ ranges from 0.37 (OLMo ladder) to 0.57 (Gadre et al., RefinedWeb)
across the seven sweep--corpus technologies, and $M^*(10^{21})$ from 3.4 to 60 tokens per parameter; even the
model-free minima paths have allocation exponents of 0.49 on Chinchilla, 0.50 on Llama~3 and 0.35--0.43 on Marin's
corpora. $M^*$ is a level: it depends on the ratio $A/B$, which absorbs every difference in tokenizers, parameter counts
and recipes. Counting Farseer's embedding parameters moves $a$ from 0.53 to 0.41 and $M^*(10^{23})$ from 19.5 to 45. On
the 975 released runs of \citet{porian2024resolving}, the allocation exponent falls from 0.84 in Kaplan et al.'s setup
to 0.50 through two familiar problems of production-function estimation: input measurement, here the counting of the
output head \citep{collardwexler2016production}, and the re-tuning of flexible inputs
\citep{ackerberg2015identification,gandhi2020identification} (Online Appendix~\ref{app:add-measurement}).

Data quality moves allocation through one channel. Better data can lower the irreducible loss, scale reducible loss
neutrally, or tilt the ratio $A/B$; only the tilt changes $M^*$ and the wedge. Across Gadre et al.'s three web corpora,
all evaluated on C4, a neutral scale with corpus-specific asymptotes is not rejected ($p=0.90$), but with little power.
DataDecide's 25 recipes reject every restriction at the resolution of the bootstrap, yet the tilt adds only 0.05 points
to the 99.7 percent of cross-recipe variation in fit that asymptotes and a neutral scale explain. It is small in loss
but not in allocation: it spans 0.22--0.26 across recipes, carried by the heavily filtered DCLM recipes, which moves
$M^*$ 2.9--3.4-fold and the wedge at given inputs 1.25--1.29-fold. DataDecide measures it before the schedule ends, and
on a common validation set part of any corpus difference is distribution match (Online Appendix~\ref{app:add-tech}). In
DeepSeek's IsoFLOP experiments, too, the allocation exponent rose from 0.45 to 0.58 as data quality improved
\citep{bi2024deepseek}, and in image-text pretraining the value of filtering depends on compute
\citep{goyal2024scaling}. The allocation exponent and $M^*$ are thus properties of a recipe, a tokenizer and a counting
convention as much as of the technology.

\subsection{A Controlled Experiment}\label{sec:tech:experiment}

No public design compares two data recipes on a common output with annealed runs, so we ran our own experiment (Online
Appendix~\ref{app:sweep}). Two corpora, FineWeb-Edu and FineWeb \citep{penedo2024fineweb}, draw on the same web crawl
and differ in filtering: FineWeb-Edu keeps the documents that an educational-quality classifier rates highly. Both share
one 8,192-token tokenizer, and losses are in bits per byte. The main grid crosses eight transformers of width 128 to 640
(0.39M--49M non-embedding parameters) with token budgets from 25M to 800M: 44 endpoints per corpus, from
$2\times10^{14}$ to $7\times10^{16}$ FLOP. $M$ runs from 0.51 to 2,031 with non-embedding parameters. Each width has one constant-learning-rate trunk, from which a copy branches off for
each budget and cools down \citep{hagele2024scaling}, so every endpoint is annealed. Four-layer models trained to 3.2
billion tokens (width 128 on both corpora, width 256 on FineWeb-Edu) raise the largest $M$ to 4,063.

The largest run uses about $10^{17}$ FLOP, five orders of magnitude below Chinchilla's largest, so the experiment tests
functional form, neutrality and the direction of extrapolation errors, not production-scale levels. Nor does it extend
Farseer's range in $M$: above about 1,000 tokens per non-embedding parameter nearly every run is a width-128 model, whose
embedding is 57 to 73 percent of its parameters. What it adds is a second recipe at those ratios, a controlled contrast
in data quality, and annealed runs at the scale of Porian et al.'s.

\textit{Pre-analysis plan.} We fixed the analysis before fitting any model \citep[on such plans,
see][]{olken2015promises}. The plan was committed to the project's working repository at 03:12 (UTC+3) on 24 September
2026 (\texttt{bd5c0ad}), when part of the FineWeb-Edu grid had finished and its raw endpoint losses had appeared in run
logs, but no model had been fitted. It fixes the output; two parameter conventions, non-embedding (primary for the
extrapolation test) and total (primary for comparisons with public designs); the estimators of
subsections~\ref{sec:tech:modelfree} and~\ref{sec:tech:parametric}; a wild cluster bootstrap by width
\citep{webb2023reworking} and a seed-covariance parametric bootstrap; and six questions. Only the question on data
quality has a decision rule: the filter is ``factor-biased'' if the 95 percent interval of the tilt $\Delta\ln(A/B)$
between corpora excludes zero with the same sign on both validation sets, ``neutral'' if it contains zero on both with a
half-width below 0.10, and inconclusive otherwise. The others are a test of equal $\sigma^*$ across corpora, a signed
statistic (the slope in $\ln M$ of the log ratio of the wedge extrapolated from runs with $M\le100$ to the model-free
local wedge) and descriptive checks of noise, tuning and on-path identification.

\textit{Power and a declared deviation.} A power study from the design alone, run before the first estimation at 17:12,
changed how the experiment can be read. At a noise standard deviation of 0.005 in log loss, the smallest tilt detected
with 80 percent power is 0.05, against DataDecide's 0.22. But with eight width clusters the plan's bootstrap is
anti-conservative \citep{mackinnon2018wild}: its nominal 95 percent intervals for the tilt cover the truth 73 to 76
percent of the time (Online Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app-power}), and no scheme reaches nominal coverage for the
model-free $\sigma^*$ or for the extrapolation slope, whose null value is not zero. We therefore declared a deviation, D8, which we call the CR2 scheme: every table reports the plan's
intervals beside intervals from leverage-adjusted (CR2) cluster residuals \citep{bell2002bias}, widened for $\sigma^*$
and the slope by calibration factors, and the slope is read against its design-specific null. A conclusion is robust
only if it holds under both schemes and the seed-covariance bootstrap. D8 was written into the analysis memo at about
17:05 but committed only at 17:44 (\texttt{a5f47f8}); its timing rests on our account, and the coverage outputs that fix
its factors carry file times of 16:55 to 17:02.

\textit{Amendments.} The same commit adds Amendment~1, which maps each outcome of the curvature, neutrality and
extrapolation questions to what the abstract, the introduction and Sections~\ref{sec:tech}--\ref{sec:wedge} will say,
and moves the comparison with Table~\ref{tab:designs} from total to FLOP-effective parameters. It was written before any
FineWeb technology estimate or tilt, but after the FineWeb learning-rate calibration, matched-cell loss differences
between the corpora, and preliminary FineWeb-Edu estimates of the model-free $\sigma^*$, 0.666 in non-embedding and
0.607 in total parameters. As FLOP-effective parameters are within 3 to 7 percent of the total count here, its band for
$\sigma^*$, $[0.60,0.76]$, was drawn with FineWeb-Edu's estimate effectively known: FineWeb-Edu's arms of the curvature
and extrapolation questions are therefore exploratory, and the confirmatory content is FineWeb's arms, the test of equal
$\sigma^*$ and the neutrality question. Three mapped statements were later changed on public-design evidence alone
(Online Appendix~\ref{app:sweep}). Amendment~2, committed and pushed to the public repository at 19:04 on 25 September 2026, before
any FineWeb technology estimate, restates the map, defines when an estimate lies outside the band and fixes the
calibration factors (1.74 for $\sigma^*$, 1.75 for the slope). It also replaces D8's restricted bootstrap test of the
tilt, which its second power study found oversized (9.2 percent on one validation set) and weak (power 0.65 against
0.22), by the CR2 interval widened by 1.25. The public repository
(\url{https://github.com/yigitokar/scaling-laws-as-production-functions}), first pushed at 18:10 on 24 September
(\texttt{59c5707}), after the private commits but before any FineWeb technology estimate, carries the plan and
Amendment~1 unchanged.

Two further deviations bear on the questions. The high-$M$ runs were trimmed for time: FineWeb trains only the width-128
four-layer shape, and the tuning checks at high $M$ exist for FineWeb-Edu only and stop at 1.6 billion tokens. And the
main grid's job lists predate the neutral sets: FineWeb's widths 320 to 640 are evaluated on them afterwards from saved
weights (deviation D13) and FineWeb-Edu's widths 384 and 512 are retrained with them (Amendment~2), but FineWeb-Edu's
original grid has no saved weights, so the main-grid tilt rests on the two in-distribution validation sets. Online Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app-deviations} lists
every deviation with its time, reason and effect.

\textit{Exploratory additions.} Trunks started after the trainer was changed (\texttt{a5f47f8}) are evaluated before
each cooldown except the last (FineWeb widths 320 to 640, FineWeb-Edu's retrained widths, the seed replicates and most
high-$M$ runs), which gives unannealed and annealed losses at the same $(N,D)$ and tests the reason for excluding Porian
et al.'s estimates, on the main grid only up to $M\approx65$. Learning-rate and weight-decay variants at the high-$M$ shape
check tuning \citep{bergsma2025power,lourie2026small}.
